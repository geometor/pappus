The Hexagon Theorem Step-by-Step¶

General Projective Construction (Synagoge VII.139)¶

Let $a$ and $b$ be two distinct lines in the plane meeting at $O$, carrying triples $(A_0, A_1, A_2)$ and $(B_0, B_1, B_2)$ respectively. Connecting alternating vertices in the cyclic order

\[H = \langle A_0,\, B_1,\, A_2,\, B_0,\, A_1,\, B_2 \rangle\]

forms a self-intersecting Pappus hexagon inscribed between the two lines. Its six sides group naturally into three pairs of opposite cross-joins:

Universal Homogeneous Symbolic Proof¶

Beyond any specific rational instance, verify_pappus_symbolic() proves Pappus’s Hexagon Theorem universally over the eight-variable polynomial ring $\mathbb{Z}[a_0, a_1, a_2, b_0, b_1, b_2, m, c]$:

  1. By an affine frame choice, take carrier $a$ as $y = 0$ with homogeneous points $A_i = [a_i : 0 : 1]$, and take carrier $b$ as $y = m x + c$ with homogeneous points $B_j = [b_j : m b_j + c : 1]$ (simultaneously covering oblique lines $m \ne 0$ and parallel lines $m = 0, c \ne 0$).

  2. In $\mathbb{P}^2$, both joining two points and intersecting two lines are given by the 3D vector cross product $\times$:

    \[M_{01} = (A_0 \times B_1) \times (A_1 \times B_0), \quad M_{12} = (A_1 \times B_2) \times (A_2 \times B_1), \quad M_{20} = (A_2 \times B_0) \times (A_0 \times B_2).\]

  3. The three points $M_{01}, M_{12}, M_{20}$ are collinear if and only if their $3 \times 3$ homogeneous determinant vanishes:

    \[\begin{split}\det \begin{bmatrix} M_{01} \\ M_{12} \\ M_{20} \end{bmatrix} \equiv 0 \quad \text{in } \mathbb{Z}[a_0, a_1, a_2, b_0, b_1, b_2, m, c].\end{split}\]

    Expanding this degree-12 polynomial in SymPy cancels every monomial identically to 0—a direct computational manifestation of the commutativity of polynomial multiplication!

Homologous Type Triangles (square, circle, diamond)¶

In GEOMETOR’s pappus-3.py investigation, the three indices $\{0, 1, 2\}$ are assigned three structural types—square ($0$), circle ($1$), and diamond ($2$)—so that each cross-join meet inherits the complementary index missing from its pair:

  • Square ($0$): A0, B0, and opposite meet M12 form △square (exact area $\frac{10}{3}$).

  • Circle ($1$): A1, B1, and opposite meet M20 form △circle (exact area $\frac{21}{37}$).

  • Diamond ($2$): A2, B2, and opposite meet M01 form △diamond (exact area $\frac{111}{10}$).
