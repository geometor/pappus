The Six Permutations ($S_3$) & The Two Star Points¶

Symmetric Group Action on Six Collinear Vertices¶

A single instance of Pappus’s Hexagon Theorem fixes an ordering of the two collinear triples $(A_0, A_1, A_2)$ on carrier $a$ and $(B_0, B_1, B_2)$ on carrier $b$. Yet the nine cross-lines $\{[A_i B_j] : 0 \le i, j \le 2\}$ exist independently of how the indices on $b$ are labeled!

Hold $(A_0, A_1, A_2)$ fixed and let the symmetric group $S_3$ of order $3! = 6$ permute the labels on $b$ via $\sigma = (\sigma_0, \sigma_1, \sigma_2) \in S_3$. Each permutation inscribes a distinct hexagon

\[H_\sigma = \langle A_0,\, B_{\sigma_1},\, A_2,\, B_{\sigma_0},\, A_1,\, B_{\sigma_2} \rangle,\]

whose three opposite cross-join intersections

\[M_{01}^{(\sigma)} = [A_0 B_{\sigma_1}] \cap [A_1 B_{\sigma_0}], \quad M_{12}^{(\sigma)} = [A_1 B_{\sigma_2}] \cap [A_2 B_{\sigma_1}], \quad M_{20}^{(\sigma)} = [A_2 B_{\sigma_0}] \cap [A_0 B_{\sigma_2}]\]

determine a distinct Pappus line $p_\sigma$. Across all six elements of $S_3$, the nine cross-lines meet in 18 cross-join intersections arranged three-by-three along six Pappus lines—splitting cleanly by permutation parity into the alternating subgroup $A_3 = \{(0,1,2),\, (1,2,0),\, (2,0,1)\}$ (even, 3-cycles) and its odd coset $S_3 \setminus A_3 = \{(0,2,1),\, (1,0,2),\, (2,1,0)\}$ (odd, transpositions).

Jakob Steiner’s Theorem (1828): The Two Star Points¶

In 1828, Jakob Steiner discovered a remarkable higher-order concurrency hidden inside the six Pappus lines: they do not meet in 15 generic points, nor do all six concur at a single point. Instead, concurrency respects permutation parity:

  • The three even Pappus lines ($\sigma \in A_3$, amber) concur at a single point, the even Steiner Star point $S_+$.

  • The three odd Pappus lines ($\sigma \in S_3 \setminus A_3$, magenta) concur at a second point, the odd Steiner Star point $S_-$.

step Trace the three even ($A_3$) Pappus lines: p012, p120, and p201.
step Verify their exact concurrency at the even Star point S_+ $= \left(\frac{80}{39},\, \frac{7}{26}\right)$: $3\left(\frac{80}{39}\right) + 18\left(\frac{7}{26}\right) = \frac{80 + 63}{13} = 11$, $9\left(\frac{80}{39}\right) + 2\left(\frac{7}{26}\right) = \frac{240 + 7}{13} = 19$, and $3\left(\frac{80}{39}\right) - 8\left(\frac{7}{26}\right) = \frac{80 - 28}{13} = 4$.
step Trace the three odd ($S_3 \setminus A_3$) Pappus lines: p021, p102, and p210.
step Verify their exact concurrency at the odd Star point S_- $= \left(\frac{5}{3},\, -\frac{1}{2}\right)$, and join the two Star points along the dashed violet Steiner line S_+S_- ($12x - 6y = 23$).

GEOMETOR’s 2023 Parallel Configuration Discovery¶

In GEOMETOR’s original 2023 pappus.py exploration ($A = [(0,0), (1,0), (3,0)]$ on $y = 0$ and $B = [(0,1), (3/2, 1), (5/2, 1)]$ on $y = 1$), constructing all nine cross-lines uncovered both a subtle projective phenomenon and exact rational Star coordinates:

  • Because $[A_0 B_1]$ and $[A_1 B_2]$ share exact slope $\frac{2}{3}$, one of the 18 cross-join pairs meets at an ideal point on the line at infinity, leaving 17 finite cross-join meets in $\mathbb{Q}^2$. On the odd transposition $\sigma = (2, 1, 0)$, that parallel cross-join pair $[A_0 B_1] \parallel [A_1 B_2]$ directs its Pappus line along exact slope $\frac{2}{3}$ toward the ideal meet!

  • All three even Pappus lines meet inside the central strip at the exact rational Star point

    \[S_+ = \left(\frac{90}{73},\; \frac{38}{73}\right),\]

    while the three odd Pappus lines concur far in the first quadrant at $S_- = \left(10,\; \frac{38}{3}\right)$.
