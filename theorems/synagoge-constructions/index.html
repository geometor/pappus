Classical Synagoge Constructions (Books III, IV, V & VIII)¶

Alongside Book VII’s projective incidence canon, geometor.pappus.synagoge_demos brings four celebrated metric, affine, and isoperimetric propositions of Pappus’s Synagoge to life in 100% exact symbolic coordinates—paired directly with the four-track Greek/English Synagoge Book Codex.

2. Book IV, Prop. 1: Equiaffine Parallelogram Area Theorem¶

In Book IV, Proposition 1 Hultsch I, pp. 176–179), Pappus generalizes Elements I.47 from squares on a right triangle to arbitrary outward parallelograms erected on two sides $AB, AC$ of any triangle $\triangle ABC$. If the outer sides produced meet at $H$, then the parallelogram constructed on base $BC$ with lateral vector equal and parallel to $\overrightarrow{HA}$ has signed area identically equal to the sum of the two side parallelograms:

\[\det(C - B,\; A - H) = \det(B - A,\; u) + \det(A - C,\; v) \quad \text{in } \mathbb{Z}[x_A, y_A, x_B, y_B, x_C, y_C, u_x, u_y, v_x, v_y].\]

verify_pappus_area_symbolic() proves this universal 10-variable polynomial identity directly in SymPy, while construct_pappus_area_theorem() models both the general scalene configuration ($13 + 15 = 28$) and Euclid’s $3$-$4$-$5$ Pythagorean specialization ($9 + 16 = 25$).

3. Book IV, Props. 14–18: Infinite Arbelos Tangent Circle Chain¶

In Book IV, Propositions 14–18 (Hultsch I, pp. 208–232), Pappus demonstrates the “ancient proposition” on the shoemaker’s knife (ἄρβηλος): for an infinite chain of circles $C_1, C_2, C_3, \dots$ inscribed tangent to the three bounding semicircles and successively to one another, the center altitude $y_n$ of the $n$-th circle above baseline $AC$ is $n$ times its diameter $d_n$:

\[r_n = \frac{R\,r_1\,r_2}{r_1 r_2 + n^2 R^2 \text{ (in reduced } \lambda\text{-form)}}, \qquad y_n = n\,d_n = 2n\,r_n \qquad (n = 1, 2, 3, \dots),\]

and every center $K_n$ lies on a single fixed ellipse with foci at $O_0$ and $O_1$.

step Construct outer semicircle (O0, A) of radius $R = 3$ and inner semicircles (O1, A) ($r_1 = 2$) and (O2, D) ($r_2 = 1$) along baseline AC.
step Inscribe base circle C1 at K1 $= \left(\frac{30}{7}, \frac{12}{7}\right)$ with diameter $d_1 = \frac{12}{7}$ and altitude P1K1 $= 1 \cdot d_1$.
step Inscribe C2 at K2 $= \left(3, \frac{12}{5}\right)$ ($y_2 = 2\,d_2$), C3 at K3 $= \left(2, \frac{12}{5}\right)$ ($y_3 = 3\,d_3$), and C4 at K4 $= \left(\frac{15}{11}, \frac{24}{11}\right)$ ($y_4 = 4\,d_4$), tracing the rational focal ellipse through all centers.

4. Book V, Prop. 1 & Book VIII, Prop. 23: Honeycomb Isoperimetry & Seven Hexagons¶

In Book V (Hultsch I, pp. 304–312) and Book VIII, Proposition 23 (Hultsch III, pp. 1096–1102), Pappus proves:

  1. Around any gapless planar vertex tiled by $k \ge 3$ congruent regular $n$-gons, $k \cdot \frac{(n - 2)\pi}{n} = 2\pi \implies (n - 2)(k - 2) = 4$, uniquely admitting $(n, k) \in \{(3, 6), (4, 4), (6, 3)\}$.

  2. At equal perimeter $P$, exact areas satisfy $A_6 : A_4 : A_3 = \frac{P^2}{8\sqrt{3}} : \frac{P^2}{16} : \frac{P^2}{12\sqrt{3}} = 3\sqrt{3} : 4 : 3$, so $A_6 / A_3 = \frac{3}{2}$ and $A_6 > A_4 > A_3$.

  3. One central regular hexagon of side $s_6$ surrounded by six congruent petal hexagons has its twelve outer vertices concyclic on a circle of exact radius $R = s_6\sqrt{7}$ (by the Law of Cosines on the $120^\circ$ triangle with legs $s_6$ and $2s_6$: $R^2 = s_6^2 + 4s_6^2 + 2s_6^2 = 7s_6^2$).
