Proposition 2: Circle Exceeds Isoperimetric Regular Polygons
============================================================

.. rubric:: *Synagoge* Book V, Prop. 2 (:cite:`Hultsch I, pp. 312–314 <hultsch:1876>`; App. IV, p. 1236) · Original English Translation

.. folio:: 0248
   :page: 1236

.. enunciation::

   A **circle** is strictly greater in area than every equilateral and equiangular polygon having the same perimeter $P$: $\operatorname{Area}_{\text{circle}} = \frac{P^2}{4\pi} > \frac{1}{2} P a_n$.

.. step::

   Circumscribe about the circle a regular polygon similar to the given isoperimetric polygon. Its area equals half its perimeter multiplied by the circle's radius $R$ (:person:`Archimedes <archimedes>`, *Measurement of a Circle* Prop. 1), and its perimeter strictly exceeds the circle's circumference (*On the Sphere and Cylinder* I). Scaling down to common perimeter $P$ shrinks the polygon's apothem strictly below $R$, whence $\frac{1}{2} P R > \frac{1}{2} P a_n$.
