========================================================================
Book V · Philological & Mathematical Notes (*Zenodorus & Archimedea*)
========================================================================

.. rubric:: Historical Reception & Transmission

1. Transmission of Zenodorus's Lost Treatise
--------------------------------------------

Pappus's treatment of isoperimetric figures in Book V draws directly upon the lost treatise *On Isoperimetric Figures* by :cite:`Zenodorus <zenodorus:isoperimetric>` (c. 200–140 BCE). Zenodorus's work is preserved today through two independent channels: Book V of Pappus's *Synagoge* and Theon of Alexandria's commentary on Ptolemy's *Almagest* (Book I).

2. The Thirteen Semi-Regular Archimedean Solids
-----------------------------------------------

In the spatial continuation of Book V (Chapter 34 / Prop. 19, *Hultsch I, pp. 352–358*), Pappus preserves the **sole surviving ancient Greek catalogue of all thirteen Archimedean semi-regular polyhedra** (the truncated tetrahedron, cuboctahedron, truncated cube, truncated octahedron, rhombicuboctahedron, truncated cuboctahedron, snub cube, icosidodecahedron, truncated dodecahedron, truncated icosahedron, rhombicosidodecahedron, truncated icosidodecahedron, and snub dodecahedron)—a treatise by Archimedes that has otherwise completely perished.

3. The Modern Honeycomb Theorem
-------------------------------

The intuition of the bees celebrated by Pappus—that the hexagonal tiling divides a surface into regions of equal area with the minimum total perimeter—remained an unproven conjecture for over sixteen centuries until it was rigorously proved by Thomas C. Hales in 1999 (*Discrete & Computational Geometry* 25, 2001).
