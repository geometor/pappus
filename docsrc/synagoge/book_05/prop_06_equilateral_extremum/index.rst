Propositions 6–7: Similar Right & Isosceles Triangle Sums
=========================================================

.. rubric:: *Synagoge* Book V, Chs. 12–14 (:cite:`Hultsch I, pp. 322–328 <hultsch-1876>`) · Original English Translation

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0355
   :page: 322

.. enunciation::

   **12. 6.** Again, let :math:`AB\Gamma` and :math:`\Delta EZ` be two similar right-angled triangles having angles :math:`\Gamma` and :math:`Z` equal; I say that the square on :math:`A\Gamma` and :math:`\Delta Z` as one line equals both the square on :math:`B\Gamma` and :math:`EZ` as one line and the square on :math:`AB` and :math:`\Delta E` as one line.

.. step::

   For let :math:`EZ` be produced to :math:`H`, let :math:`EH` be placed equal to :math:`B\Gamma`, let a line drawn through :math:`H` parallel to :math:`\Delta E` meet :math:`\Delta Z` produced at :math:`K`, and through :math:`\Delta` let :math:`\Delta\Theta` be drawn parallel to :math:`ZH`.
   Since, then, :math:`\Delta\Theta` equals :math:`HE` in a parallelogram, that is, :math:`B\Gamma`, and :math:`\angle K\Delta\Theta` equals :math:`Z`, that is, :math:`\Gamma`, and right angle :math:`\Theta` equals :math:`B`, and remaining angle :math:`K` equals :math:`A`, triangles :math:`K\Theta\Delta` and :math:`AB\Gamma` are equiangular and equal;
   therefore the square on :math:`KZ` equals the squares on :math:`KH` and :math:`HZ` (:just:`I.47`), that is, the square on :math:`A\Gamma` and :math:`\Delta Z` as one line equals both the square on :math:`AB` and :math:`\Delta E` as one line and the square on :math:`B\Gamma` and :math:`EZ` as one line.

.. enunciation::

   **13. 7.** Similar isosceles triangles taken together are greater than two isosceles triangles upon the same bases that are dissimilar to each other and to the similar ones, yet :term:`isoperimetric <isoperimetros>` with them.

.. step::

   Let :math:`\Delta ZB` and :math:`BA\Gamma` be similar isosceles triangles,

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0357
   :page: 324

.. step::

   and upon the same bases let :math:`\Delta EB` and :math:`B\Lambda\Gamma` be other isosceles triangles isoperimetric with :math:`\Delta ZB` and :math:`BA\Gamma`, yet necessarily dissimilar [because their angles are unequal];
   and that it is possible to construct this will be shown; I say that :math:`\Delta BZ` and :math:`BA\Gamma` together are greater than :math:`\Delta EB` and :math:`B\Lambda\Gamma` together. Let :math:`EZ` and :math:`A\Lambda` be joined, and let them be produced to the bases;
   then they will cut them in two and at right angles; for :math:`\Delta E` and :math:`EZ` equal :math:`BE` and :math:`EZ`, and bases :math:`\Delta Z` and :math:`ZB` are equal because the triangles are isosceles, and angles are equal [and triangles :math:`\Delta EZ` and :math:`ZEB` are similar],
   so that the exterior angles at :math:`Z` are also equal [being equal to the interior ones], and angles :math:`\Delta` and :math:`B` are equal, and the remaining angles at :math:`H` are equal; therefore they are right angles, and :math:`\Delta H` equals :math:`HB`.
   Likewise :math:`BM` and :math:`M\Gamma` are equal, and the angles at :math:`M` are right angles. Let them cut, then, at :math:`H` and :math:`M`, let :math:`EH` be produced, let :math:`H\Theta` be placed equal to it, and let :math:`\Theta B` be joined;
   then :math:`\angle EBH` will equal :math:`\angle \Theta BH`. But :math:`\angle EBH` is greater than :math:`\angle AB\Gamma` (because it is greater than :math:`\angle ZBH`, which equals it, since triangles :math:`\Delta BZ` and :math:`BA\Gamma` are similar); therefore :math:`\angle \Theta BH` is also greater than :math:`\angle AB\Gamma`;
   therefore the line joining points :math:`\Theta` and :math:`\Lambda` cuts :math:`BM`, since :math:`\Delta B\Gamma` is a straight line and :math:`\Theta BN` produced lies outside :math:`AB`, because :math:`\angle AB\Gamma` is less than :math:`\angle \Theta BH`, that is, its vertical angle :math:`\angle NB\Gamma`,
   and far more is :math:`\angle \Lambda B\Gamma` less, so that :math:`BM` is cut by :math:`\Theta\Lambda` at :math:`K` [and it is manifest that it does not cut :math:`M\Gamma`, lest when :math:`\Lambda M` is produced it should cut it at a point other than :math:`\Lambda`].

.. step::

   **14.** Since, then, :math:`\Delta EB` and :math:`B\Lambda\Gamma` equal :math:`\Delta ZB` and :math:`BA\Gamma` (for they are supposed

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0359
   :page: 326

.. step::

   :term:`isoperimetric <isoperimetros>`), their halves :math:`EB` and :math:`B\Lambda`, that is, :math:`\Theta B` and :math:`B\Lambda`, also equal :math:`ZB` and :math:`BA`;
   and :math:`\Theta B` and :math:`B\Lambda` are greater than :math:`\Theta\Lambda`, so :math:`ZB` and :math:`BA` are also greater than :math:`\Theta\Lambda`. Therefore the square on :math:`ZB` and :math:`BA` together as one line is greater than the square on :math:`\Theta\Lambda`.
   But the square on :math:`ZB` and :math:`BA` together as one line equals the square on :math:`ZH` and :math:`AM` as one line together with the square on :math:`HB` and :math:`BM` together as one line, that is, the square on :math:`HM`, because of the similarity of right-angled triangles :math:`HZB` and :math:`BAM` (for this was proved beforehand);
   while the square on :math:`\Theta\Lambda`, that is, the square on :math:`\Theta K` and :math:`K\Lambda` together as one line, equals the square on :math:`\Lambda M` and :math:`H\Theta` together as one line, that is, the square on :math:`\Lambda M` and :math:`HE` as one line,
   together with the square on :math:`HK` and :math:`KM` together as one line, that is, the square on :math:`HM`, by the same theorem proved beforehand. Therefore the square on :math:`AM` and :math:`ZH` as one line together with the square on :math:`HM` is greater than the square on :math:`EH` and :math:`\Lambda M` as one line together with the square on :math:`HM`.
   Let the common square on :math:`HM` be subtracted; the remaining square on :math:`ZH` and :math:`AM` as one line is greater than the square on :math:`HE` and :math:`\Lambda M` as one line; and therefore in length :math:`ZH` and :math:`AM` as one line is greater than :math:`EH` and :math:`\Lambda M` as one line.
   Now triangles under the same height are to one another as their bases (:just:`VI.1`); therefore as :math:`HE` is to :math:`HZ`, so are the halves of the triangles :math:`EHB` to :math:`ZHB` and the whole triangles [their doubles] :math:`\Delta EB` to :math:`\Delta ZB`, while as :math:`\Lambda M` is to :math:`MA`, so is :math:`M\Lambda\Gamma` to :math:`MA\Gamma`,
   and the double :math:`B\Lambda\Gamma` to :math:`BA\Gamma`. *Componendo*, therefore,

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0361
   :page: 328

.. step::

   triangles :math:`\Delta EB` and :math:`B\Lambda\Gamma` have to triangles :math:`\Delta ZB` and :math:`AB\Gamma` the same ratio as :math:`EH` and :math:`\Lambda M` combined to :math:`ZH` and :math:`AM` (for this also follows immediately). And :math:`EH` and :math:`\Lambda M` together are less than :math:`ZH` and :math:`AM` together; therefore triangles :math:`\Delta EB` and :math:`B\Lambda\Gamma` together are also less than triangles :math:`\Delta ZB` and :math:`BA\Gamma` together.
