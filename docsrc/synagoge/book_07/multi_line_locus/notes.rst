Notes: Multi-Line Locus & Pappus–Guldinus Theorem
=================================================

.. rubric:: Philological & Historical Commentary

1. The Birth of Analytic Geometry (Descartes 1637)
--------------------------------------------------

In Book I of :cite:`La Géométrie (1637) <descartes:geometrie>`, :person:`René Descartes <descartes>` took Pappus's exact statement of the multi-line locus problem as the launching pad for coordinate geometry. Descartes noted that while Pappus recorded that Euclid and Apollonius could not solve the problem for more than 4 lines, his algebraic coordinates $(x, y)$ could express the locus for 5, 6, or any arbitrary number of lines as algebraic curves of degree $\lceil n/2 \rceil$.

2. Paul Guldin's Rediscovery and Authenticity
--------------------------------------------------

:person:`Paul Guldin <guldin>` published the theorem in Book II of his *Centrobaryca* (Vienna, 1640). Although seventeenth-century contemporaries initially questioned whether the passage was an interpolation in :cite:`Commandino's 1588 translation <commandino:1588>`, the primary manuscript witness *Codex Vaticanus Graecus 218* (*fol. 165r*) definitively confirms the Greek text as authentic Pappus (*Hultsch II, p. 682*).
