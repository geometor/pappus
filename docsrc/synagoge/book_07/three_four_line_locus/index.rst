The Three-, Four-, and Multi-Line Locus Problem
===============================================

.. rubric:: *Synagoge* Book VII, Chs. 33–40 (:cite:`Hultsch II, pp. 676–680 <hultsch-1877>`) · Original English Translation

.. folio:: hultsch_1877_vol2:0221
   :page: 676

**33.** Thus far :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>`. As for the locus on three and four lines which he says in the third book was not completed by :person:`Euclid <euclid>`, neither he himself nor anyone else would have been able [nor even to add a small part to what was written by Euclid] by means of
only the conics already demonstrated up to the time of Euclid, as he himself bears witness when he says that it is impossible for it to be completed without what he was compelled to set out beforehand. **34.** [Now Euclid, accepting :person:`Aristaeus <aristaeus>` as worthy on account of the conics he had already handed down, and not anticipating or not wishing

.. folio:: hultsch_1877_vol2:0223
   :page: 678

to lay down the same treatment of them—being most fair and kindly toward all who were able to advance mathematics even to any degree, and in no way quarrelsome, and exact yet not boastful like this man—wrote as much of the locus as was possible by means of Aristaeus's conics, without saying that what was shown was complete;
for then it would have been necessary to convict him, whereas now it is not at all so, since he himself also is not censured for leaving most things incomplete in the *Conics*. **35.** He was able to add what was lacking to the locus because he had before his mind what had already been written by Euclid concerning the locus
and had studied for a very long time with the pupils of Euclid in Alexandria, whence he acquired that habit of mind, not unlearned. Now this locus on three and four lines, on which he prides himself, adding that thanks are due to the first writer, is as follows.]

**36.** If, three straight lines being given in position, straight lines be drawn from some [the same] point to the three at given angles, and the ratio of the rectangle contained by two of the drawn lines to the square on the remaining one be given, the point will touch a solid locus given in position, that is, one of the three conic lines.
And if straight lines be drawn to four straight lines given in position at given angles, and the ratio of the rectangle contained by two of the drawn lines to that contained by the remaining two be given, in like manner the point will touch a conic section given in position. **37.** [For to two lines alone, the locus has been shown to be plane;]
but if to more than four, the point will touch loci no longer known, but called merely lines [of what kind or having what properties is no longer known], of which not even one

.. folio:: hultsch_1877_vol2:0225
   :page: 680

that seemed most evident have they composed and shown to be useful.

**38.** Their enunciations are these: if from some point straight lines be drawn to five straight lines given in position at given angles, and the ratio of the rectangular solid parallelepiped contained by three of the drawn lines to the rectangular solid parallelepiped contained by the remaining two drawn lines and some given line be given,
the point will touch a line given in position. And if to six, and the ratio of the aforesaid solid contained by three to that contained by the remaining three be given, again the point will touch a line given in position. But if to more than six, they can no longer say
if a ratio is given of something contained by the four to that contained by the rest, since there is nothing contained by more than three dimensions.

**39.** Yet those shortly before us have allowed themselves to interpret such things, signifying nothing intelligible at all, speaking of that which is contained by these
multiplied by the square on this or by that contained by these. Yet it was possible by means of compound ratios both to state and to prove these things universally both for the aforesaid propositions and for these in the following way:

**40.** if from some point straight lines be drawn to straight lines given in position
at given angles, and the ratio compounded from that which one drawn line has to one, and another to another, and another to another, and the remaining one to a given line if there be seven, or if eight, the remaining one to the remaining one,
be given, the point will touch a line given in position; and likewise however many there be, odd or even in number. While these follow, as I said, after the locus on four lines, they have not put together even one of them so that the line is known.
