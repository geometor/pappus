Four Constructions for Two Mean Proportionals
=============================================

.. rubric:: *Synagoge* Book III, Chapters 22–27 (:cite:`Hultsch I, pp. 56–68 <hultsch-1876>`) · Original English Translation

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0089
   :page: 56

**22.** We shall therefore set forth four constructions of it with some elaboration of my own: first that of :person:`Eratosthenes <eratosthenes>`, second that of :person:`Nicomedes <nicomedes>`, third that of :person:`Hero <heron>`, which is especially suited to manual practice for those who wish to build, and last the one discovered by us.
[For of any solid, another solid similar to the given one is constructed in the given ratio if two mean proportionals in continued proportion be taken between two given straight lines, as :person:`Hero <heron>` says in his :cite:`Mechanics <heron-mechanica>` and Catapult Treatises.]

**23.** Let :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta` be a fixed rectangular frame, and in it equal right-angled triangles :math:`AE\Theta`, :math:`MZK`, :math:`NH\Lambda`, having right angles at :math:`E`, :math:`Z`, :math:`H`. Let :math:`AE\Theta` remain fixed, while :math:`MZK` moves along rulers :math:`AB` and :math:`\Gamma\Delta` so that :math:`MZ` slides along ruler :math:`AB`, grooved throughout, and vertex :math:`K`

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0091
   :page: 58

through ruler :math:`\Gamma\Delta`, itself grooved along its whole length; similarly let triangle :math:`NH\Lambda` move along rulers :math:`AB` and :math:`\Gamma\Delta` in the aforesaid grooves. With these things thus laid down, when someone wishes to make a cube double a cube, taking :math:`A\Gamma` double :math:`\Lambda\Xi` and separating triangles :math:`MZK` and :math:`NH\Lambda`
until points :math:`A`, :math:`\Xi` become collinear with the intersections :math:`\Pi`, :math:`O` of the triangles, he will join :math:`A\Pi O\Xi`, meeting :math:`\Gamma\Delta` at :math:`P` (for this must necessarily follow), and thus what is proposed comes about for him.
For since :math:`A\Gamma : \Pi\Theta = AP : \Pi P = A\Theta : \Pi K = \Theta P : PK = \Pi\Theta : OK = \Pi P : PO = \Pi K : O\Lambda = KP : P\Lambda = OK : \Lambda\Xi`, therefore :math:`\Pi\Theta`, :math:`OK` are two means between :math:`A\Gamma`, :math:`\Lambda\Xi` in continued proportion.
And :math:`A\Gamma` is double :math:`\Lambda\Xi`; therefore the cube on :math:`A\Gamma` is also double the cube on :math:`\Pi\Theta`. But if the cube has another ratio to the cube, :math:`A\Gamma` must have that ratio to :math:`\Lambda\Xi`, and construct the rest similarly. [And from this it is evident that it is impossible to solve the proposed problem by plane means.]

**24.** According to :person:`Nicomedes <nicomedes>`, two means in continued proportion between two given straight lines :math:`\Gamma\Delta`, :math:`\Delta A` are taken in this manner.

Let parallelogram :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta` be completed, and let each of :math:`AB`, :math:`B\Gamma` be bisected at points :math:`\Lambda`, :math:`E`, and let :math:`A\Delta` joined be produced and meet :math:`\Gamma B`

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0093
   :page: 60

produced at :math:`H`, and :math:`EZ` perpendicular to :math:`B\Gamma`, and let :math:`\Gamma Z` be joined equal to :math:`A\Lambda`, and let :math:`ZH` be joined, and :math:`\Gamma\Theta` parallel to it; and since the angle :math:`K\Gamma\Theta` exists, from the given point :math:`Z` let :math:`Z\Theta K` be drawn through making :math:`\Theta K` equal to :math:`A\Lambda` or :math:`\Gamma Z`
(for this was shown to be possible through the conchoid line), and let :math:`K\Lambda` joined be produced and meet :math:`BA` produced at :math:`M`; I say that :math:`\Delta\Gamma : \Gamma K = \Gamma K : MA = MA : A\Delta`.

For since :math:`B\Gamma` is bisected at :math:`E` and :math:`\Gamma K` is added to it, the rectangle :math:`BK, K\Gamma` together with the square on :math:`\Gamma E` equals the square on :math:`EK`. Let the square on :math:`EZ` be added in common;
therefore the rectangle :math:`BK, K\Gamma` together with the squares on :math:`\Gamma E, EZ`, that is the square on :math:`\Gamma Z`, equals the squares on :math:`KE, EZ`, that is the square on :math:`KZ`. And since :math:`MA : AB = M\Lambda : \Lambda K`, while :math:`M\Lambda : \Lambda K = B\Gamma : \Gamma K`, therefore :math:`MA : AB = B\Gamma : \Gamma K`.
And half of :math:`AB` is :math:`A\Lambda`, while double :math:`B\Gamma` is :math:`\Gamma H`; therefore :math:`MA : A\Lambda = H\Gamma : \Gamma K`. But :math:`\Gamma H : \Gamma K = Z\Theta : \Theta K` because of the parallels

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0095
   :page: 62

:math:`HZ`, :math:`\Gamma\Theta`; therefore *componendo*, :math:`M\Lambda : A\Lambda = ZK : K\Theta`. And :math:`A\Lambda` is supposed equal to :math:`\Theta K` [since :math:`A\Lambda` is also equal to :math:`\Gamma Z`]; therefore :math:`M\Lambda` is also equal to :math:`ZK`. Therefore the square on :math:`M\Lambda` is also equal to the square on :math:`ZK`.
And the square on :math:`M\Lambda` equals the rectangle :math:`BM, MA` together with the square on :math:`A\Lambda`, while the square on :math:`ZK` was shown equal to the rectangle :math:`BK, K\Gamma` together with the square on :math:`\Gamma Z`, of which the square on :math:`A\Lambda` equals the square on :math:`\Gamma Z` (for :math:`A\Lambda` is supposed equal to :math:`\Gamma Z`);
therefore the remaining rectangle :math:`BM, MA` equals the remaining rectangle :math:`BK, K\Gamma`. Therefore :math:`MB : BK = \Gamma K : MA`. But :math:`MB : BK = \Delta\Gamma : \Gamma K`; therefore :math:`\Delta\Gamma : \Gamma K = \Gamma K : AM`. And also :math:`MB : BK = MA : A\Delta`; therefore :math:`\Delta\Gamma : \Gamma K = K\Gamma : AM = MA : A\Delta`.

**25.** According to :person:`Hero <heron>`, how it is possible to take two mean proportionals between two given straight lines instrumentally, we shall show, since this problem is, as :person:`Hero <heron>` also says, solid. "We shall set forth," he says, "of the demonstrations the one most suitable for manual practice."

**26.** For let the given straight lines :math:`AB`, :math:`B\Gamma` be placed at right angles to one another, between which two mean proportionals must be found. Let parallelogram :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta` be completed, and let :math:`\Delta\Gamma`, :math:`\Delta A` be produced, and let :math:`\Delta B`, :math:`\Gamma A` be joined, and let a ruler be placed at point :math:`B` and moved

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0097
   :page: 64

cutting :math:`\Gamma E`, :math:`AZ`, until the line drawn from :math:`H` to the cut on :math:`\Gamma E` equals that from :math:`H` to the cut on :math:`AZ`. Let it be done, and let the ruler's position be :math:`EBZ`, with :math:`EH`, :math:`HZ` equal; I say then that :math:`AZ`, :math:`\Gamma E` are mean proportionals between :math:`AB`, :math:`B\Gamma`.

For since parallelogram :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta` is rectangular, the four straight lines :math:`\Delta H, HA, HB, H\Gamma` are equal to one another. Since then :math:`\Delta H` equals :math:`AH` and :math:`HZ` is drawn through, the rectangle :math:`\Delta Z, ZA` together with the square on :math:`AH` equals the square on :math:`HZ`.
For the same reasons, the rectangle :math:`\Delta E, E\Gamma` together with the square on :math:`\Gamma H` equals the square on :math:`HE`. And :math:`HE`, :math:`HZ` are equal; therefore the rectangle :math:`\Delta Z, ZA` together with the square on :math:`AH` equals the rectangle :math:`\Delta E, E\Gamma` together with the square on :math:`\Gamma H`, of which the square on :math:`\Gamma H` equals the square on :math:`HA`;
therefore the remaining rectangle :math:`\Delta E, E\Gamma` equals the remaining rectangle :math:`\Delta Z, ZA`. Therefore :math:`E\Delta : \Delta Z = ZA : \Gamma E`. But :math:`E\Delta : \Delta Z = BA : AZ` and :math:`E\Gamma : \Gamma B`, so that :math:`AB : AZ = ZA : \Gamma E = \Gamma E : \Gamma B`; therefore :math:`AZ`, :math:`\Gamma E` are mean proportionals between :math:`AB`, :math:`B\Gamma`.

**27.** A cube double a cube is not only found through the underlying instrument also according to us, but also universally one having the prescribed ratio.

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0099
   :page: 66

For let semicircle :math:`AB\Gamma` be constructed, and from center :math:`\Delta` let :math:`\Delta B` be erected at right angles, and let a ruler move about point :math:`A` so that one end of it rests against a pin standing at point :math:`A`, while the remaining part moves about the pin as center between :math:`B` and :math:`\Gamma`.
With these thus constructed, let it be prescribed to find two cubes having a given ratio to one another. And let the ratio of :math:`B\Delta` to :math:`\Delta E` be made the same as the given ratio, and let :math:`\Gamma E` joined be produced to :math:`Z`. Now let the ruler be brought across between :math:`B` and :math:`\Gamma`
until the part of it intercepted between straight lines :math:`ZE, EB` becomes equal to the part between straight line :math:`BE` and circumference :math:`BK\Gamma`; for by continually trying and shifting the ruler we shall easily do this. Let it be done, and let it have position :math:`AH\Theta K`, so that :math:`H\Theta, \Theta K` are equal.
I say that the cube on :math:`B\Delta` has to the cube on :math:`\Delta\Theta` the prescribed ratio, that is the ratio of :math:`\Delta B` to :math:`\Delta E`.

For let the circle be conceived as completed, and let :math:`K\Delta` joined be produced to :math:`\Lambda`, and let :math:`\Lambda H` be joined;
it is therefore parallel to :math:`B\Delta` because :math:`K\Theta` equals :math:`\Theta H` and :math:`K\Delta` equals :math:`\Delta\Lambda`. Let :math:`A\Lambda` and :math:`\Lambda\Gamma` also be joined. Since then angle :math:`HA\Lambda` in a semicircle is right and :math:`AM` is perpendicular, therefore :math:`\Lambda M^2 : MA^2`, that is :math:`\Gamma M : MA`, equals :math:`AM^2 : MH^2`
(for also :math:`\Lambda M : MA = MA : MH`, so that :math:`\Lambda M^2 : MA^2 = AM^2 : MH^2 = \Gamma M : MA`). Let the ratio :math:`AM : MH` be added in common. Therefore the ratio compounded of

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0101
   :page: 68

:math:`\Gamma M : MA` and :math:`AM : MH`, that is the ratio :math:`\Gamma M : MH`, is the same as the ratio compounded of :math:`AM^2 : MH^2` and :math:`AM : MH`. And the ratio compounded of :math:`AM^2 : MH^2` and :math:`AM : MH` is the same as the ratio which the cube on :math:`AM` has to the cube on :math:`MH`;
therefore also the ratio :math:`\Gamma M : MH` is the same as the ratio of the cube on :math:`AM` to the cube on :math:`MH`. But :math:`\Gamma M : MH = \Gamma\Delta : \Delta E`, that is :math:`B\Delta : \Delta E`, while :math:`AM : MH = A\Delta : \Delta\Theta`, that is :math:`\Delta B : \Delta\Theta`;
and therefore :math:`B\Delta : \Delta E`, that is the given ratio, equals the cube on :math:`B\Delta` to the cube on :math:`\Delta\Theta`. If then we also make :math:`B\Delta : \Delta\Theta = \Delta\Theta : \Delta N`, then :math:`\Delta\Theta`, :math:`\Delta N` will be two mean proportionals between :math:`B\Delta`, :math:`\Delta E`.
