Delian Cube Duplication and Two Mean Proportionals
==================================================

.. rubric:: *Synagoge* Book III, Chs. 22–27 / Props. 1–5 (:cite:`Hultsch I, pp. 56–68 <hultsch:1876>`; App. IV, p. 1217) · Original English Translation

.. folio:: 0229
   :page: 1217

Because conic sections are delicate to draw directly in the plane by hand, the ancients also devised exact mechanical and :term:`neusis <neusis>` constructions for inserting **two mean proportionals** ($a : x = x : y = y : b$) between two given straight lines (*Chs. 22–27, Props. 1–5, Hultsch I pp. 56–68*):

1. **The Mesolabe of** :person:`Eratosthenes <eratosthenes>` (*Prop. 1*): Three sliding rectangular or trapezoidal congruent triangles shifted along parallel rulers until intersecting vertices align collinearly on a common transversal ray.
2. **The Conchoidal Verging Ruler of** :person:`Nicomedes <nicomedes>` (*Prop. 2*): An auxiliary triangle extended by a marked rule pivoted through a fixed pole so that the intercept cut off between an asymptote and a given line equals a prescribed length (:term:`neusis <neusis>`).
3. **The Rectangle-and-Pivot Method of** :person:`Hero of Alexandria <heron>` (*Prop. 3*): A graduated straightedge pivoted around the corner of a rectangle $a \times b$ until equal segments fall on the extended adjacent sides.
4. **Pappus's Own Semicircular Construction (*Prop. 4–5*)**: Turning a straightedge around one endpoint of a semicircle until its chord and interior prolongation stand in the desired proportion—equivalent in geometric effect to Hero's method while adapting seamlessly to planar surveying instruments.
