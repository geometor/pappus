Address to Pandrosion · Summary & Methodological Architecture
=============================================================

.. rubric:: Epistemological & Classification Framework

1. The Theorem vs. Problem Distinction
---------------------------------------

Pappus opens Book III with a fundamental epistemological distinction:

* **Theorem** (*θεώρημα*): Demonstrates a necessary consequence from given hypotheses. Its truth is universal and unconditional once hypotheses are granted.
* **Problem** (*πρόβλημα*): Demands the physical or geometric construction of an entity not yet existing. It requires a :term:`diorismos <diorismos>` (*διορισμός*) defining existence conditions, uniqueness, and multiplicity.

2. The Three Genera of Loci (*Τρία Γένη*)
-----------------------------------------

Pappus articulates the canonical Alexandrian hierarchy of geometric problems:

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 20 30 50

   * - Genus
     - Instruments / Curves
     - Canonical Problems & Treatises
   * - **Plane** (*ἐπίπεδα*)
     - Straight edge & compass (straight lines & circles)
     - *Elements* Books I–VI; constructions generated within a single plane.
   * - **Solid** (*στερεά*)
     - Conic sections (parabola, hyperbola, ellipse)
     - Duplication of the cube (Delian problem $a : x = x : y = y : b$), trisection of an arbitrary angle; requires conical surfaces.
   * - **Linear** (*γραμμικά*)
     - Higher kinematic & mechanical curves
     - Archimedean spiral, quadratrix of Dinostratus, conchoid of Nicomedes, cissoid of Diocles.

3. The Methodological Prohibition of Genus-Crossing
---------------------------------------------------

Pappus formalizes the ancient methodological canon: solving a problem using tools from an improper genus is a major geometric error (*ἁμάρτημα οὐ μικρόν*):

* Solving a **plane problem** using conic sections or higher curves wastes geometric elegance and demonstrates incompetence.
* Attempting to force a **solid problem** (such as angle trisection or cube duplication) into straight lines and circles alone is impossible, as the problem inherently transcends plane degree.
