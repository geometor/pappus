Address to Pandrosion · Philological & Textual Notes
====================================================

.. rubric:: Manuscript Transmission & Historical Persona

1. Pandrosion of Alexandria
---------------------------

The addressee of Book III, :person:`Pandrosion <pandrosion>`, is addressed in *Codex Vaticanus Graecus* 218 (f. 1r) as ``ὦ κρατίστη Πανδροσίων``. The feminine superlative ``κρατίστη`` (“most excellent” / “most noble”) confirms that Pandrosion was a prominent female mathematical educator in early fourth-century Alexandria—flourishing roughly two generations prior to Hypatia (c. 355–415 CE).

* In the 1588 editio princeps, :cite:`Federico Commandino <commandino:1588>` amended ``κρατίστη`` to masculine ``κράτιστε`` under the assumption that an Alexandrian geometer could only be male.
* :cite:`Friedrich Hultsch <hultsch:1876>` restored the manuscript reading, confirmed by modern philological scholarship (e.g., Knorr 1989, Cuomo 2000).

2. The Delian Cube-Duplication Controversy
------------------------------------------

Pappus vigorously criticizes a member of Pandrosion's circle who claimed to have discovered a plane construction for two mean proportionals. Pappus dissects the student's construction across Chapters 2–19, showing how the purported solution fails to meet the requisite continuous proportion $a : x = x : y = y : b$.
