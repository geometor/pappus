Proposition 14: The Ancient Theorem on the Arbelos
==================================================

.. rubric:: *Synagoge* Book IV, Ch. 19 / Prop. 14 (:cite:`Hultsch I, pp. 208–210 <hultsch:1876>`; App. IV, p. 1225) · Original English Translation

.. folio:: 0237
   :page: 1225

.. enunciation::

   Let outer semicircle :g:`(O0, A) <g3:( O0 A )>` on diameter :g:`AC <g3:[ A C ]>` be divided at :g:`D <g3:D>` into inner semicircles :g:`(O1, A) <g3:( O1 A )>` of radius $r_1$ and :g:`(O2, D) <g3:( O2 D )>` of radius $r_2$ ($R = r_1 + r_2$). Inscribe circles :g:`C1 <g3:( K1 X1 )>`, :g:`C2 <g3:( K2 X2 )>`, :g:`C3 <g3:( K3 X3 )>`, :g:`C4 <g3:( K4 X4 )>`, $\dots$ mutually tangent in the :term:`arbelos <arbelos>`, and drop altitudes :g:`P1K1 <g3:[ P1 K1 ]>`, :g:`P2K2 <g3:[ P2 K2 ]>`, :g:`P3K3 <g3:[ P3 K3 ]>`, :g:`P4K4 <g3:[ P4 K4 ]>` perpendicular to :g:`AC <g3:[ A C ]>`. Then for every $n \ge 1$, the altitude $y_n = |P_n K_n|$ equals $n$ times the diameter $d_n$ of circle $C_n$: $y_n = n\,d_n$.

Pappus explicitly notes (*Hultsch I p. 208*) that the theorem remains equally valid whether the two inner semicircles lie internally side-by-side (forming the classical shoemaker's knife) or embrace one another externally, before introducing three preparatory lemmas (*Props. 15–17*) to prove the result synthetically.
