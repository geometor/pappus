Proposition 14: The Ancient Theorem on the Arbelos
==================================================

.. rubric:: *Synagoge* Book IV, Ch. 19 / Prop. 14 (:cite:`Hultsch I, p. 208 <hultsch-1876>`) · Original English Translation

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0241
   :page: 208

.. enunciation::

   **19. (14)** There is handed down among certain writers an ancient proposition of the following kind: let three semicircles tangent to one another be supposed, :g:`ABG <g3:( O0 A )>`, :g:`ADE <g3:( O1 A )>`, and :g:`EZG <g3:( O2 D )>`,
   and into the space between their circumferences, which they call the :term:`arbelos <arbelos>`, let circles be inscribed tangent both to the semicircles and to one another, as many as one may please, such as those about centers :g:`H <g3:K1>`, :g:`T <g3:K2>`, :g:`K <g3:K3>`, :g:`L <g3:K4>`;
   to show that the perpendicular from center :g:`H <g3:K1>` upon :g:`AG <g3:[ A C ]>` equals the diameter of the circle about :math:`H`, the perpendicular from :g:`T <g3:K2>` equals double the diameter of the circle about :math:`\Theta`,
   the perpendicular from :g:`K <g3:K3>` equals triple its diameter, and the succeeding perpendiculars are multiples of their own diameters according to the successive numbers exceeding one another by unity as the inscription of circles proceeds without end. First, however, the lemmas assumed shall be proved.
