Propositions 10–11: Archimedes's Lever Saying and Hero's *Baroulkos*
====================================================================

.. rubric:: *Synagoge* Book VIII, Propositions 10–11 (:cite:`Hultsch III, pp. 1060–1073 <hultsch:1878>`) · Critical English Edition

.. folio:: 0069
   :page: 1060

.. enunciation::

   **19 (Prop. 11).** To the same theory belongs also the moving of a given weight by a given power; for this is said to be a mechanical discovery of :person:`Archimedes <archimedes>`, upon which he is said to have uttered the saying: *"Give me,"* says he, *"where to stand, and I will move the earth."*

.. picture:: 0071.g1.png
   :alt: Archival woodcut plate 0071.g1 — Hero's Baroulkos compound cogwheel train perspective engraving (Hultsch III, p. 1062)
   :caption: Original Hultsch bitmap (folio 0071, p. 1062): perspective engraving of Hero's Baroulkos compound reduction gear train.

And :person:`Hero of Alexandria <heron>` has set forth its construction very clearly in the work called :term:`Baroulkos <baroulkos>`, taking a lemma which he demonstrated in his *Mechanics*, where he also treats of the five powers—namely the wedge, lever, screw, pulley-block, and axle-in-wheel—by which the given weight is moved by the given power [according to each power].

Now in the *Baroulkos*, by the juxtaposition of toothed drums, he moved the given weight by the given power, the diameter of the drum having to the diameter of the axle the ratio which :math:`5` has to :math:`1`, the moved weight being supposed :math:`1{,}000` talents and the moving power :math:`5` talents.

**20.** Let us then demonstrate the same thing in a double ratio, with the moved weight being :math:`160` talents instead of :math:`1{,}000`, and the power moving it being supposed :math:`4` talents

.. folio:: 0071
   :page: 1062

instead of :math:`5`—that is, let the man who moves it be able by himself without a machine to draw :math:`4` talents.
And let the casing described by him be :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta`, and in it, along the long parallel walls, let an axle :math:`EZ` be set turning freely; and fixed to this let there be a toothed drum [with toothed spokes] :math:`H\Theta`, having its diameter double the diameter [of the diagonal :math:`EZ`]
of the axle at its squared section [for it is made square about the middle for such a length as is the thickness of the drum into which it fits securely, and somewhat rounded or tapered in the parts on either side of the drum].
If, then, the ropes tied from the weight being drawn [which are called cables] be wound through an opening [or rather a wide slot] in wall :math:`AB` around the axle :math:`EZ` [on either side of drum :math:`H\Theta`] and drum :math:`H\Theta` be turned, this will also turn the attached axle

.. folio:: 0073
   :page: 1064

moving about its ends in bronze rings
and sockets likewise of bronze [movable], resting in the aforesaid walls :math:`AB`, :math:`\Gamma\Delta`. And the cables [which are called the load] winding up from the weight will move the weight. And in order that drum :math:`H\Theta` be moved, it will be necessary to apply a power of more than :math:`80` talents,
because the diameter of the drum is double the diameter of the axle; for this is a problem demonstrated by Hero in his *Mechanics* [and very many other most useful and serviceable problems have been written].

**21.** Since, therefore, we do not have the given power of :math:`80` talents, but of :math:`4` talents,
let another axle :math:`K\Lambda` be set parallel alongside :math:`EZ`, having fixed to it a toothed drum :math:`MN`, so that its teeth fit into the teeth of drum :math:`H\Theta`; and this comes about if, as the diameter of drum :math:`H\Theta` is to the diameter of :math:`MN`,
so is the number of teeth of :math:`H\Theta` to the number of teeth of :math:`MN` (how this is done will be clear from what follows); drum :math:`MN` is therefore also given. And to the same axle :math:`K\Lambda` let drum :math:`\Xi O` be fixed, having its diameter double the diameter of drum :math:`MN`.
Therefore he who wishes to move the weight through drum :math:`\Xi O` will need a power of :math:`40` talents, since :math:`80` talents are double :math:`40` talents.

**22.** Again, alongside drum :math:`\Xi O` [being toothed] let

.. folio:: 0075
   :page: 1066

another toothed drum :math:`\Pi P` be placed, fixed to another axle,
and to the same axle another fixed drum :math:`\Sigma T`, having likewise its diameter double the diameter of drum :math:`\Pi P`, with its teeth not interlocking with the teeth of drum :math:`MN`; the power moving the weight through drum :math:`\Sigma T` will therefore be :math:`20` talents.
Now the given power was :math:`4` talents; it will be necessary, then, that another toothed drum :math:`Y\Phi` be placed alongside :math:`\Sigma T` [being toothed], and fixed to the axle of drum :math:`Y\Phi` let there be toothed :math:`X\Psi`, whose diameter has to the diameter of drum :math:`Y\Phi` the ratio of :math:`2` to :math:`1`;
the power moving the weight through drum :math:`X\Psi` will therefore be :math:`10` talents. Again, let another toothed drum be placed alongside drum :math:`X\Psi`, and fixed to its axle let there be a drum :math:`M^\alpha M^\beta` toothed with oblique teeth, whose diameter has to the diameter of that drum the ratio which :math:`10` talents have to the :math:`4` talents of the given power.

**23.** These having been constructed, if we conceive the casing :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta` suspended steadfastly, and hang the weight from axle :math:`EZ` and the drawing power of :math:`4` talents from drum :math:`M^\alpha M^\beta`, neither of them will descend, since the axles turn freely and the arrangement of the drums fits accurately,
but as upon a balance the power of :math:`4` talents will equilibrate with the weight of :math:`160` talents. If, then, we add to one of them some small weight, it will incline and be moved toward whichever side the addition has been made;
for if, for example, a weight of one mina be added to the power of :math:`4` talents, overpowering it will draw up the weight of :math:`160` talents.

**24.** Instead of this addition let a screw

   :page: 1068

   :alt: Archival woodcut plate 0074.g1 — Baroulkos gear train with worm screw and crank handle (Hultsch III, p. 1067)
   :caption: Original Hultsch bitmap (folio 0074, p. 1067): Hero's Baroulkos showing worm screw and crank handle.

:math:`\Omega A` be placed alongside drum :math:`M^\alpha M^\beta`, having its helix fitting the oblique teeth of drum :math:`M^\alpha M^\beta`. How this is to be made has been written in the same *Mechanics* of :person:`Hero <heron>`, and we too shall set this out more clearly in what follows.
And let the screw turn freely on pivots resting in round holes, one of which projects to the outside of the casing at wall :math:`\Gamma\Delta`, and let the projection, having been squared, receive the crank-handle :math:`\varsigma B`,
by grasping and turning which we shall turn both the screw and drum :math:`M^\alpha M^\beta`, and consequently also :math:`\varsigma\Pi` fixed to it. On this account :math:`X\Psi` alongside it will also turn, and :math:`Y\Phi` fixed to it, and :math:`\Sigma T` alongside it, and :math:`\Pi P` fixed to this, and :math:`\Xi O` alongside this,
and :math:`MN` fixed to this, and :math:`H\Theta` alongside this, and consequently also axle :math:`EZ` fixed to it, around which by winding the cables from the load we shall move the weight. For that it will move is clear from the fact that another power has been added,
namely that of the crank-handle, which describes a circle greater than the perimeter of the screw; for it was demonstrated in the *On Balances* of :person:`Archimedes <archimedes>` and the *Mechanics* of Philo and :person:`Hero <heron>` that greater circles overpower lesser circles whenever their rotation takes place about the same center.

**25 (Prop. 12).** These things, then, would be what chiefly holds together the theoretical part of mechanics. Of the instrumental branch there are many species

.. folio:: 0079
   :page: 1070

and divisions: for some things contemplated by reason in mechanics, gnomonics, and the treatise on water-clocks are shown as constructed through instruments themselves,
while many others are accomplished externally even apart from mechanical devices, and some problems difficult to manage by geometrical methods this art takes over with instruments and brings to an easier construction. Straightway, for instance, the so-called Delian problem, being solid by nature, could not be constructed by following geometrical reasoning,
since neither was it easy to draw the sections of a cone in a plane; yet when taken over by instruments into manual operation and construction it is suitably reduced [rather than as set out by the others thus] [the proposed problem],
I mean to find a cube double a cube. And not only is the double found by means of the underlying instrument, but universally one having the assigned ratio.

**26.** For let a semicircle :math:`AB\Gamma` be constructed, and from center :math:`\Delta` let :math:`\Delta B` be drawn upright at right angles,
and let a ruler be moved about point :math:`A` in such a way that one end of it fits around a peg fixed at point :math:`A`, while the remaining part moves as about the peg as center between :math:`B, \Gamma`.
When these have been constructed, let it be assigned to find two cubes having to each other a given ratio, and let the ratio of :math:`B\Delta` to :math:`\Delta E` be made the same as that ratio, and let :math:`\Gamma E` joined be produced to :math:`Z`.
Let the ruler then be moved alongside between :math:`B, \Gamma`, until the part of it intercepted between straight lines :math:`ZE, EB` becomes equal to the part

.. folio:: 0081
   :page: 1072

between straight line :math:`BE` and circumference :math:`BK\Gamma`; for by continually trying and shifting the ruler we shall easily do this.
Let it then be done, and let it have position :math:`AH\Theta K`, so that :math:`H\Theta, \Theta K` are equal; I say that the cube on :math:`B\Delta` has to the cube on :math:`\Delta\Theta` the assigned ratio, that is, the ratio of :math:`B\Delta` to :math:`\Delta E`.

For let the circle be conceived completed, and let :math:`K\Delta` joined be produced to :math:`\Lambda`, and let :math:`\Lambda H` be joined; it is therefore parallel to :math:`B\Delta` because :math:`K\Theta` is equal to :math:`\Theta H` and :math:`K\Delta` to :math:`\Delta\Lambda`. Let :math:`A\Lambda` and :math:`\Lambda\Gamma` then be joined.
Since, then, angle :math:`HA\Lambda` in a semicircle is right and :math:`AM` is perpendicular, it is therefore as the square on :math:`\Lambda M` to the square on :math:`MA`, that is as :math:`\Gamma M` to :math:`MA`, so the square on :math:`AM` to the square on :math:`MH`.
Let the ratio of :math:`AM` to :math:`MH` be added in common; therefore the ratio compounded of the ratio of :math:`\Gamma M` to :math:`MA` and that of :math:`AM` to :math:`MH`, that is the ratio of :math:`\Gamma M` to :math:`MH`, is the same as the ratio compounded
of the square on :math:`AM` to the square on :math:`MH` and the ratio of :math:`AM` to :math:`MH`. But the ratio compounded of the square on :math:`AM` to the square on :math:`MH` and of :math:`AM` to :math:`MH` is the same as the ratio which the cube on :math:`AM` has to the cube on :math:`MH`;
therefore the ratio of :math:`\Gamma M` to :math:`MH` is the same as the ratio of the cube on :math:`AM` to the cube on :math:`MH`. But as :math:`\Gamma M` is to :math:`MH`, so is :math:`\Gamma\Delta` to :math:`\Delta E`, that is :math:`B\Delta` to :math:`\Delta E`, and as :math:`AM` is to :math:`MH`, so is :math:`A\Delta` to :math:`\Delta\Theta`,
that is :math:`\Delta B` to :math:`\Delta\Theta`; and therefore as :math:`B\Delta` is to :math:`\Delta E`, that is as the given ratio, so is the cube on :math:`B\Delta` to the cube on :math:`\Delta\Theta`.
