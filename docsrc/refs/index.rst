References
==========

Primary Ancient & Classical Sources
-----------------------------------

- **Pappus of Alexandria** (c. 340 CE). *Book 7 of the Collection* (*Synagoge*, *Topos
  Analomenos*). Edited with translation and commentary by **Alexander Jones** (2 vols.,
  Sources in the History of Mathematics and Physical Sciences 8). New York: Springer-Verlag,
  1986. *(See especially Lemmas XI, XIII, XX, XXI = Book VII Propositions 129, 131, 138, 139 on
  Euclidean Porisms.)*
- **Commandino, Federico** (1588). *Pappi Alexandrini Mathematicae Collectiones a Federico
  Commandino Urbinate in Latinum conversae, et commentariis illustratae*. Pisauri (Pesaro).
  *(The Renaissance Latin edition through which Descartes, Desargues, Pascal, and Newton
  encountered Pappus's Hexagon Lemma.)*
- **Hultsch, Friedrich** (1876–1878). *Pappi Alexandrini Collectionis quae supersunt* (3 vols.).
  Berlin: Weidmann.

Modern Projective, Algebraic & Constructive Geometry
----------------------------------------------------

- **Steiner, Jakob** (1832). *Systematische Entwickelung der Abhängigkeit geometrischer
  Gestalten von einander*. Berlin: Fincke. *(Establishes the parity concurrency of the six
  $S_3$ Pappus lines at the two Steiner points and investigates complete-quadrilateral harmonic
  ranges.)*
- **Hilbert, David** (1899). *Grundlagen der Geometrie*. Leipzig: Teubner. *(Chapter VI proves
  that Pappus's Hexagon Theorem in a projective plane is logically equivalent to the
  commutativity of multiplication in its coordinate division ring.)*
- **Coxeter, H. S. M.** (1987). *Projective Geometry* (2nd ed.). New York: Springer-Verlag.
  *(Comprehensive synthetic treatment of perspectivities, projectivities, harmonic conjugates,
  Pappus's configuration $9_3$, and Steiner's theorem.)*
- **Richter-Gebert, Jürgen** (2011). *Perspectives on Projective Geometry: A Guided Tour Through
  Real and Complex Geometry*. Berlin: Springer. *(Homogeneous cross-product bracket algebra and
  automated theorem proving for incidence configurations.)*
- **Wildberger, Norman J.** (2005). *Divine Proportions: Rational Trigonometry to Universal
  Geometry*. Sydney: Wild Egg. *(Defines algebraic quadrance $Q(A,B)$ and line spread $s(l_1,l_2)$
  over arbitrary fields without transcendental functions.)*
