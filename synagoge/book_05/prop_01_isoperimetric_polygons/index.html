Proposition 1: Isoperimetric Regular Polygons¶

Synagoge Book V, Ch. 4 / Prop. 1 (Hultsch I, pp. 308–310) · Original English Translation

4. 1. Let \(AB\Gamma\) and \(\Delta EZ\) be two equilateral and equiangular polygons, and let their perimeters be equal, and let \(\Delta EZ\) have the greater number of angles; I say that \(\Delta EZ\) is greater than \(\Gamma AB\).
step For let \(H\) and \(\Theta\) be taken as the centers of the circles circumscribed about them, let perpendiculars \(HK\) and \(\Theta\Lambda\) be drawn, and let \(AH\), \(H\Gamma\), \(\Theta\Delta\), and \(\Theta Z\) be joined. Since \(E\Delta Z\) has more angles than \(AB\Gamma\), \(\Delta Z\) measures the perimeter of polygon \(\Delta EZ\) more times than \(A\Gamma\) measures that of \(AB\Gamma\). Therefore \(A\Gamma\) is greater than \(\Delta Z\) (for the perimeters are supposed equal), so that \(AK\) is also greater than \(\Delta\Lambda\) (each being half of its respective side). Let \(KM\) be placed equal to \(\Delta\Lambda\), and let \(MH\) be joined. And since whatever part the straight line \(A\Gamma\) is of the perimeter of polygon \(AB\Gamma\), that same part is the angle \(AH\Gamma\) of four right angles (because the polygon is equilateral), and likewise whatever part \(\Delta Z\) is of the perimeter of \(\Delta EZ\), that same part is angle \(\Delta\Theta Z\) of four right angles, and the perimeters are equal to each other and four right angles to four right angles, as \(A\Gamma\) is to the perimeter of \(AB\Gamma\), so is angle \(H\) to four right angles; and as the perimeter of \(\Delta EZ\), that is, of \(AB\Gamma\), is to \(\Delta Z\), so are four right angles to angle \(\Delta\Theta Z\).
f. 0343 · p. 310
step Ex aequali, therefore, as \(A\Gamma\) is to \(\Delta Z\), so is \(\angle AH\Gamma\) to \(\angle \Delta\Theta Z\); and therefore as \(AK\) is to \(\Delta\Lambda\), that is, to \(KM\), so is \(\angle AHK\) to \(\angle \Delta\Theta\Lambda\). Now \(AK\) has to \(KM\) a greater ratio than \(\angle AHK\) has to \(\angle MHK\) (for this has been proved in the Lemmas on Spherics); therefore \(\angle AHK\) also has to \(\angle \Delta\Theta\Lambda\) a greater ratio than \(\angle AHK\) has to \(\angle MHK\); therefore \(\angle MHK\) is greater than \(\angle \Delta\Theta\Lambda\). And the right angle at \(K\) equals the right angle at \(\Lambda\); therefore the remaining angle \(\angle HMK\) is less than \(\angle \Theta\Delta\Lambda\). Let \(\angle KMN\) be constructed equal to \(\angle \Theta\Delta\Lambda\); and \(\Delta\Lambda\) equals \(KM\); therefore \(\Lambda\Theta\) equals \(KN\); therefore \(\Theta\Lambda\) is greater than \(KH\). And the perimeters are equal; therefore the rectangle contained by \(\Lambda\Theta\) and the perimeter of \(\Delta EZ\) is greater than the rectangle contained by \(KH\) and the perimeter of \(AB\Gamma\). And the polygons are halves of the aforesaid regions; therefore polygon \(\Delta EZ\) is greater than \(AB\Gamma\). [For the altitude is equal since the perimeter of the two rectilinear figures is the same, and the bases \(\Theta\Lambda\) and \(HK\) are unequal, and as base \(\Theta\Lambda\) is to base \(HK\), so is the parallelogram under \(\Theta\Lambda\) and the perimeter of \(\Delta EZ\) to the parallelogram under \(HK\) and the perimeter of \(AB\Gamma\); and their halves, the polygons, are unequal, so that \(\Delta EZ\) is greater than \(AB\Gamma\).]
