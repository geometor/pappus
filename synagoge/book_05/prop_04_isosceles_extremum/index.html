Proposition 4: Isosceles Triangle Area Extremum¶

Synagoge Book V, Ch. 11 / Prop. 5 (Hultsch I, pp. 318–322) · Original English Translation

11. 5. Among isoperimetric triangles having the same base, the isosceles triangle is greatest, and the more isosceles is always greater.
step For upon base \(B\Gamma\) let there be isoperimetric triangles, \(AB\Gamma\) being isosceles, and \(B\Delta\Gamma\) more isosceles than \(BE\Gamma\) (for it is possible to construct this by what was just proved above); I say that \(AB\Gamma\) is greatest, and \(B\Delta\Gamma\) is greater than \(BE\Gamma\).
f. 0353 · p. 320
step Let \(BA\) be produced, let \(AZ\) be placed equal to \(\Gamma A\), and let \(Z\Delta\) and \(\Delta A\) be joined. Since \(Z\Delta\) and \(\Delta B\) are greater than \(BZ\), they are also greater than \(BA\) and \(A\Gamma\) (for \(A\Gamma\) equals \(AZ\)). But \(BA\) and \(A\Gamma\) equal \(B\Delta\) and \(\Delta\Gamma\); therefore \(B\Delta\) and \(\Delta Z\) are greater than \(B\Delta\) and \(\Delta\Gamma\). Subtracting the common segment \(B\Delta\), the remainder \(Z\Delta\) is greater than \(\Delta\Gamma\). Now the two lines \(ZA\) and \(A\Delta\) equal the two lines \(\Gamma A\) and \(A\Delta\), each to each, and base \(Z\Delta\) is greater than base \(\Delta\Gamma\); therefore \(\angle ZA\Delta\) is greater than \(\angle \Delta A\Gamma\) ([I.25]); therefore \(\angle ZA\Gamma\) is more than double \(\angle \Delta A\Gamma\). And it is double \(\angle AB\Gamma\), that is, \(\angle A\Gamma B\) (for the triangle is isosceles); therefore \(\angle A\Gamma B\) is greater than \(\angle \Delta A\Gamma\). Let \(\angle \Gamma AH\) be constructed equal to it; therefore \(AH\) is parallel to \(B\Gamma\) because the alternate angles are equal ([I.27]). Hence, when \(\Gamma\Delta\) is produced to \(H\) and \(BH\) is joined, it is manifest that triangle \(AB\Gamma\) is greater than \(B\Delta\Gamma\); for \(BA\Gamma\) equals \(BH\Gamma\) ([I.37]).
step Again, let \(B\Delta\) be produced to \(K\), let \(\Delta K\) be placed equal to \(\Delta\Gamma\), and let \(KE\) and \(\Delta E\) be joined. Since \(BE\) and \(EK\) are greater than \(BK\), that is, than \(B\Delta\) and \(\Delta\Gamma\), that is, than \(BE\) and \(E\Gamma\), subtracting the common segment \(BE\), the remainder \(EK\) is greater than \(E\Gamma\). Now the two lines \(K\Delta\) and \(\Delta E\) equal the two lines \(\Gamma\Delta\) and \(\Delta E\), each to each, and base \(KE\) is greater than base \(E\Gamma\); therefore \(\angle K\Delta E\) is greater than \(\angle \Gamma\Delta E\); therefore \(\angle K\Delta\Gamma\) is more than double \(\angle \Gamma\Delta E\). And the same angle \(\angle K\Delta\Gamma\) is less than double \(\angle \Delta\Gamma B\) (for \(\angle \Delta\Gamma B\) is greater than \(\angle \Delta B\Gamma\); for \(\angle AB\Gamma\) and \(\angle A\Gamma B\) are equal); therefore \(\angle \Delta\Gamma B\) is greater than \(\angle \Gamma\Delta E\). Let \(\angle \Gamma\Delta\Lambda\) be constructed on \(\Delta\Gamma\) at \(\Delta\) equal to \(\angle \Delta\Gamma B\);
f. 0355 · p. 322
step for it is manifest that \(\Delta\Lambda\) will fall between \(\Delta E\) and \(\Delta K\), being parallel to \(B\Gamma\) because the alternate angles are equal ([I.27]). When \(\Gamma E\) is produced to meet parallel \(\Delta\Lambda\) at \(\Lambda\) and \(B\Lambda\) is joined, triangle \(B\Gamma\Delta\) will equal \(B\Lambda\Gamma\) [upon the same base \(\Gamma B\) and in the parallels \(B\Gamma\) and \(\Delta\Lambda\)], so that triangle \(AB\Gamma\) is greater than \(BE\Gamma\), while \(B\Delta\Gamma\) is less than \(AB\Gamma\).
