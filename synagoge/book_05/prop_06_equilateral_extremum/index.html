Propositions 6–7: Similar Right & Isosceles Triangle Sums¶

Synagoge Book V, Chs. 12–14 (Hultsch I, pp. 322–328) · Original English Translation

12. 6. Again, let \(AB\Gamma\) and \(\Delta EZ\) be two similar right-angled triangles having angles \(\Gamma\) and \(Z\) equal; I say that the square on \(A\Gamma\) and \(\Delta Z\) as one line equals both the square on \(B\Gamma\) and \(EZ\) as one line and the square on \(AB\) and \(\Delta E\) as one line.
step For let \(EZ\) be produced to \(H\), let \(EH\) be placed equal to \(B\Gamma\), let a line drawn through \(H\) parallel to \(\Delta E\) meet \(\Delta Z\) produced at \(K\), and through \(\Delta\) let \(\Delta\Theta\) be drawn parallel to \(ZH\). Since, then, \(\Delta\Theta\) equals \(HE\) in a parallelogram, that is, \(B\Gamma\), and \(\angle K\Delta\Theta\) equals \(Z\), that is, \(\Gamma\), and right angle \(\Theta\) equals \(B\), and remaining angle \(K\) equals \(A\), triangles \(K\Theta\Delta\) and \(AB\Gamma\) are equiangular and equal; therefore the square on \(KZ\) equals the squares on \(KH\) and \(HZ\) ([I.47]), that is, the square on \(A\Gamma\) and \(\Delta Z\) as one line equals both the square on \(AB\) and \(\Delta E\) as one line and the square on \(B\Gamma\) and \(EZ\) as one line.
13. 7. Similar isosceles triangles taken together are greater than two isosceles triangles upon the same bases that are dissimilar to each other and to the similar ones, yet isoperimetric with them.
step Let \(\Delta ZB\) and \(BA\Gamma\) be similar isosceles triangles,
f. 0357 · p. 324
step and upon the same bases let \(\Delta EB\) and \(B\Lambda\Gamma\) be other isosceles triangles isoperimetric with \(\Delta ZB\) and \(BA\Gamma\), yet necessarily dissimilar [because their angles are unequal]; and that it is possible to construct this will be shown; I say that \(\Delta BZ\) and \(BA\Gamma\) together are greater than \(\Delta EB\) and \(B\Lambda\Gamma\) together. Let \(EZ\) and \(A\Lambda\) be joined, and let them be produced to the bases; then they will cut them in two and at right angles; for \(\Delta E\) and \(EZ\) equal \(BE\) and \(EZ\), and bases \(\Delta Z\) and \(ZB\) are equal because the triangles are isosceles, and angles are equal [and triangles \(\Delta EZ\) and \(ZEB\) are similar], so that the exterior angles at \(Z\) are also equal [being equal to the interior ones], and angles \(\Delta\) and \(B\) are equal, and the remaining angles at \(H\) are equal; therefore they are right angles, and \(\Delta H\) equals \(HB\). Likewise \(BM\) and \(M\Gamma\) are equal, and the angles at \(M\) are right angles. Let them cut, then, at \(H\) and \(M\), let \(EH\) be produced, let \(H\Theta\) be placed equal to it, and let \(\Theta B\) be joined; then \(\angle EBH\) will equal \(\angle \Theta BH\). But \(\angle EBH\) is greater than \(\angle AB\Gamma\) (because it is greater than \(\angle ZBH\), which equals it, since triangles \(\Delta BZ\) and \(BA\Gamma\) are similar); therefore \(\angle \Theta BH\) is also greater than \(\angle AB\Gamma\); therefore the line joining points \(\Theta\) and \(\Lambda\) cuts \(BM\), since \(\Delta B\Gamma\) is a straight line and \(\Theta BN\) produced lies outside \(AB\), because \(\angle AB\Gamma\) is less than \(\angle \Theta BH\), that is, its vertical angle \(\angle NB\Gamma\), and far more is \(\angle \Lambda B\Gamma\) less, so that \(BM\) is cut by \(\Theta\Lambda\) at \(K\) [and it is manifest that it does not cut \(M\Gamma\), lest when \(\Lambda M\) is produced it should cut it at a point other than \(\Lambda\)].
step 14. Since, then, \(\Delta EB\) and \(B\Lambda\Gamma\) equal \(\Delta ZB\) and \(BA\Gamma\) (for they are supposed
f. 0359 · p. 326
step isoperimetric), their halves \(EB\) and \(B\Lambda\), that is, \(\Theta B\) and \(B\Lambda\), also equal \(ZB\) and \(BA\); and \(\Theta B\) and \(B\Lambda\) are greater than \(\Theta\Lambda\), so \(ZB\) and \(BA\) are also greater than \(\Theta\Lambda\). Therefore the square on \(ZB\) and \(BA\) together as one line is greater than the square on \(\Theta\Lambda\). But the square on \(ZB\) and \(BA\) together as one line equals the square on \(ZH\) and \(AM\) as one line together with the square on \(HB\) and \(BM\) together as one line, that is, the square on \(HM\), because of the similarity of right-angled triangles \(HZB\) and \(BAM\) (for this was proved beforehand); while the square on \(\Theta\Lambda\), that is, the square on \(\Theta K\) and \(K\Lambda\) together as one line, equals the square on \(\Lambda M\) and \(H\Theta\) together as one line, that is, the square on \(\Lambda M\) and \(HE\) as one line, together with the square on \(HK\) and \(KM\) together as one line, that is, the square on \(HM\), by the same theorem proved beforehand. Therefore the square on \(AM\) and \(ZH\) as one line together with the square on \(HM\) is greater than the square on \(EH\) and \(\Lambda M\) as one line together with the square on \(HM\). Let the common square on \(HM\) be subtracted; the remaining square on \(ZH\) and \(AM\) as one line is greater than the square on \(HE\) and \(\Lambda M\) as one line; and therefore in length \(ZH\) and \(AM\) as one line is greater than \(EH\) and \(\Lambda M\) as one line. Now triangles under the same height are to one another as their bases ([VI.1]); therefore as \(HE\) is to \(HZ\), so are the halves of the triangles \(EHB\) to \(ZHB\) and the whole triangles [their doubles] \(\Delta EB\) to \(\Delta ZB\), while as \(\Lambda M\) is to \(MA\), so is \(M\Lambda\Gamma\) to \(MA\Gamma\), and the double \(B\Lambda\Gamma\) to \(BA\Gamma\). Componendo, therefore,
f. 0361 · p. 328
step triangles \(\Delta EB\) and \(B\Lambda\Gamma\) have to triangles \(\Delta ZB\) and \(AB\Gamma\) the same ratio as \(EH\) and \(\Lambda M\) combined to \(ZH\) and \(AM\) (for this also follows immediately). And \(EH\) and \(\Lambda M\) together are less than \(ZH\) and \(AM\) together; therefore triangles \(\Delta EB\) and \(B\Lambda\Gamma\) together are also less than triangles \(\Delta ZB\) and \(BA\Gamma\) together.
