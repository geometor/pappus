Glossary¶

Lexicon of all 16 technical Greek and Latin concepts across Books II–VIII of Pappus's Synagoge. Every :term: reference in the Codex resolves directly to its anchor below.

synagoge (συναγωγή) · Lat. collectio mathematica — Collection / Mathematical Synthesis

Pappus’s eight-book compendium gathering, repairing, generalizing, and supplying missing lemmas across seven centuries of classical and Hellenistic Greek geometry and mechanics. Hultsch loci: I–III passim.

topos analyomenos (ὁ καλούμενος ἀναλυόμενος τόπος) · Lat. locus resolutus — Treasury / Domain of Analysis

Special body of 33 advanced Greek geometric books (by Euclid, Apollonius, Aristaeus, and Eratosthenes) equipping the geometer, after mastering the Elements, with the power to discover solutions to proposed problems via analysis and synthesis. Hultsch loci: II pp. 634–638.

analysis (ἀνάλυσις) · Lat. resolutio / analysis — geometric analysis

Assuming that which is sought as if already admitted, and advancing through its consequences to something already known or ranked among first principles; divided by Pappus into theoretical (ζητητικόν) and problematic (ποριστικόν) analysis. Hultsch loci: II pp. 634–636.

synthesis (σύνθεσις) · Lat. compositio / synthesis — geometric synthesis

Reversing the path of analysis: taking as starting point the last principle reached in analysis, ordering its antecedents in their natural deductive sequence, and arriving at the construction or proof of what was sought. Hultsch loci: II pp. 634–636.

diorismos (διορισμός) · Lat. determinatio — determination / condition of possibility

Necessary and sufficient criterion distinguishing when, how, and in how many ways (πότε καὶ πῶς καὶ ποσαχῶς) a proposed geometric problem admits a solution. Hultsch loci: II pp. 636, 642–650.

porisma (πόρισμα) · Lat. porisma — porism

According to the ancients (as defended by Pappus against later redefinitions), something proposed for the finding (πορισμός) of the very thing proposed—intermediate between a theorem (proving an attribute) and a problem (constructing a figure). Hultsch loci: II pp. 648–660.

lemma (λῆμμα) · Lat. lemma / assumptum — auxiliary lemma

Auxiliary geometric proposition supplied by Pappus across Book VII (242 lemmas in total) to bridge tacit steps in the analytical treatises of Euclid and Apollonius—including the Hexagon Theorems VII.138–139. Hultsch loci: II pp. 684–1014.

epipedos / stereos / grammikos (ἐπίπεδα, στερεά, γραμμικὰ προβλήματα) · Lat. problemata plana, solida, linearia — plane, solid, and linear problems

Pappus’s canonical threefold methodological hierarchy (Books III & IV): plane problems solved by straight lines and circles alone; solid problems requiring sections of the cone; and linear problems requiring higher curves (spirals, quadratrices, conchoids, cissoids). Hultsch loci: I pp. 54–56, 270–272.

mesotes (μεσότης) · Lat. medietas — proportional mean (arithmetic, geometric, harmonic)

Ordered triple $(a, m, b)$ governed by an invariant difference or ratio law; in Book III Pappus exhibits the three classical means $A = \frac{a+b}{2}$, $G = \sqrt{ab}$, $H = \frac{2ab}{a+b}$ simultaneously within a single semicircle and catalogs all ten ancient medieties. Hultsch loci: I pp. 68–105.

harmonike mesotes / diairesis (ἁρμονικὴ μεσότης) · Lat. divisio harmonica — harmonic mean & harmonic division

Four collinear points $(A, C; D, T)$ or segments where $AB : BC = AD : DC$, equivalent to cross-ratio $(A, C; D, T) = -1$; unites Book III’s semicircle mean construction with Book VII Prop. 131’s complete-quadrilateral harmonic range. Hultsch loci: I pp. 70–83; II p. 874.

neusis (νεῦσις) · Lat. inclinatio — verging construction (neusis)

Inserting a straight segment of given length between two given lines or curves so that its prolongation passes through (“verges toward”, νεύει) a given pole; subject of Apollonius’s Inclinations and Book IV’s angle-trisection analyses. Hultsch loci: I pp. 242–250, 272–285; II pp. 670, 778–824.

arbelos (τὸ καλούμενον ἄρβηλον) · Lat. arbelus / scalprum sutorium — arbelos (shoemaker’s knife)

Curvilinear region bounded by three mutually tangent semicircles on a common diameter; in Book IV Props. 14–18 Pappus proves the “ancient proposition” (ἀρχαία πρότασις) that the $n$-th inscribed tangent circle has center altitude $h_n = n \cdot d_n$. Hultsch loci: I pp. 208–232.

isoperimetros (ἰσοπερίμετρος) · Lat. isoperimeter — isoperimetric (of equal perimeter)

Having equal bounding perimeter (in the plane) or equal bounding surface area (in space); Book V proves that among regular tiling polygons of equal perimeter, the bees’ regular hexagon encloses strictly greater area than the square or equilateral triangle. Hultsch loci: I pp. 304–350.

kentron barous (κέντρον τοῦ βάρους) · Lat. centrum gravitatis — center of gravity (centroid)

Unique point within or associated with a body or plane figure such that, if suspended from that point, the figure remains in equilibrium in every orientation; foundation of both the Pappus–Guldinus revolution rule (VII, p. 682) and Book VIII mechanics (VIII, p. 1030). Hultsch loci: II p. 682; III pp. 1030–1048.

baroulkos (βαρουλκός) · Lat. barulcus — Baroulkos (Hero’s geared weight-hauler)

Hero of Alexandria’s compound reduction gear-train (τύμπανα ὀδοντωτά) coupled with an endless screw (κοχλίας), presented in Synagoge VIII to move a given great weight ($1{,}000$ talents) by a small given force ($5$ talents). Hultsch loci: III pp. 1060–1068, 1104–1115.

heni diastemati (τὰ ἑνὶ διαστήματι γραφόμενα) · Lat. uno circini intervallo descripta — single-opening (“rusty”) compass constructions

Instrumental geometric problems solved in Synagoge Book VIII (p. 1074 ff.) using a fixed compass aperture that may neither expand nor contract, inaugurating the fixed-compass tradition later celebrated by Abu’l-Wafa, Tartaglia, Cardano, and Poncelet–Steiner. Hultsch loci: III pp. 1074–1078.
