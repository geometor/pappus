Bibliography & Primary Sources¶

Critical register of 100% public-domain primary editions, Renaissance and modern translations, classical treatises summarized in the Synagoge, and projective descendants. Every :cite: reference in the Codex resolves directly to its anchor below.

Primary Editions & Public-Domain Commentaries on Pappus¶

Pappus of Alexandria (ed. Friedrich Hultsch) — Pappi Alexandrini Collectionis quae supersunt, Vol. I (Books II–V) (Berlin: Weidmann, 1876, pp. 2–473) [1876]

Key: hultsch:1876 Standard critical Greek edition with parallel Latin translation and apparatus criticus from Codex Vaticanus Graecus 218 for Books II (Apollonian tetrads), III (Pandrosion & Three Means), IV (Area Theorem, Arbelos & Higher Curves), and V (Honeycomb Isoperimetry & 13 Archimedean Solids). CTS URN: urn:cts:greekLit:tlg2032.tlg001.1st1K-grc1. Provenance: https://archive.org/details/pappialexandrini01pappuoft

Pappus of Alexandria (ed. Friedrich Hultsch) — Pappi Alexandrini Collectionis quae supersunt, Vol. II (Books VI–VII) (Berlin: Weidmann, 1877, pp. 474–1016) [1877]

Key: hultsch:1877 Standard critical Greek edition of Book VI (Little Astronomy) and Book VII (Treasury of Analysis: Preface to Hermodorus, 3/4/$n$-Line Locus, Pappus–Guldinus Centroid Theorem, Cross-Ratio VII.129, Harmonics VII.131, Hexagon Theorems VII.138–139, Focus–Directrix VII.238). Provenance: https://archive.org/details/pappialexandrini02pappuoft

Pappus of Alexandria (ed. Friedrich Hultsch) — Pappi Alexandrini Collectionis quae supersunt, Vol. III (Book VIII & Index Graecitatis) (Berlin: Weidmann, 1878, pp. 1022–1289) [1878]

Key: hultsch:1878 Standard critical Greek edition of Book VIII (Mechanics, Inclined Plane, Archimedes’s Lever Saying, Hero’s Baroulkos, Single-Opening Compass Constructions, Five-Point Conic, Seven Hexagons in a Circle, and Toothed Gearwheels). Provenance: https://archive.org/details/pappialexandrini03pappuoft

Federico Commandino (trans.) — Pappi Alexandrini Mathematicae Collectiones a Federico Commandino Urbinate in Latinum conversae et commentariis illustratae (Pesaro: Hieronymus Concordia, 1588; reissued Venice 1589, Bologna 1660) [1588]

Key: commandino:1588 Monumental Renaissance Latin editio princeps through which Descartes, Guldin, Desargues, Fermat, Pascal, and Newton read Pappus nearly three centuries before Hultsch’s Greek edition.

Sir Thomas Little Heath — A History of Greek Mathematics, Vol. II: From Aristarchus to Diophantus (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1921, Ch. XIX: Pappus of Alexandria, pp. 355–439) [1921]

Key: heath:1921 Comprehensive public-domain mathematical analysis of Books II–VIII of Pappus’s Collection. Provenance: https://archive.org/details/historyofgreekma02heat

Sir Thomas Little Heath — The Thirteen Books of Euclid’s Elements (2nd ed., 3 vols., Cambridge University Press, 1926; geometor/euclid) [1926]

Key: heath:1926 Primary Euclidean commentary in geometor/euclid, cross-citing Pappus’s Synagoge across all eight pappus:collectio* keys.

Ancient Mathematical & Mechanical Treatises in the Synagoge¶

Euclid — Elements (Στοιχεῖα, Books I–XIII) (Alexandria, c. 300 BCE; geometor/euclid & geometor/elements) [c. 300 BCE]

Key: euclid:elements Axiomatic foundation generalized and supplemented across Pappus’s Synagoge (IV.1 generalizing I.47 & VI.31; III.5–16 extending VI.13; III.54–58 complementing XIII.13–18).

Euclid — Data (Δεδομένα) (First treatise of the Treasury of Analysis, Synagoge VII, Hultsch II pp. 636–638) [c. 300 BCE]

Key: euclid:data 90 propositions on magnitudes given in magnitude, ratio, species, or position; heads Pappus’s 33-book analytical curriculum.

Euclid — Porisms (Πορίσματα, 3 books — lost) (apud Pappum, Synagoge VII, Hultsch II pp. 648–660 & Lemmas VII.127–164, pp. 866–918) [c. 300 BCE]

Key: euclid:porisms Lost three-book masterpiece of 171 propositions in 29 genera; Pappus’s 38 lemmas to the Porisms include Cross-Ratio Invariance (VII.129), Harmonic Division (VII.131), and the Hexagon Theorems (VII.138–139).

Euclid — Surface Loci (Τόποι πρὸς ἐπιφανείᾳ, 2 books — lost) (apud Pappum, Synagoge VII, Hultsch II p. 636 & Lemmas VII.235–242, pp. 1004–1014) [c. 300 BCE]

Key: euclid:surface-loci Lost two-book treatise whose final Pappus lemmas (VII.238, Hultsch II p. 1008) preserve the earliest surviving proof of the Focus–Directrix eccentricity property of conic sections.

Apollonius of Perga — Conics (Κωνικά, 8 books) (Synagoge VII, Hultsch II pp. 672–682 & Lemmas VII.165–234, pp. 918–1004) [c. 200 BCE]

Key: apollonius:conics Culmination of solid-locus geometry in the Treasury of Analysis; discussed by Pappus in connection with the Three- and Four-Line Locus and the Pappus–Guldinus Centroid Theorem.

Apollonius of Perga — Cutting Off of a Ratio, Cutting Off of an Area, Determinate Section, Tangencies, Inclinations & Plane Loci (12 books) (apud Pappum, Synagoge VII, Hultsch II pp. 636, 640–672 & Lemmas VII.1–126, pp. 684–866) [c. 200 BCE]

Key: apollonius:cutting-off-ratio Six two-book Apollonian analytical treatises forming the central trunk of Pappus’s Treasury of Analysis.

Aristaeus the Elder — Five Books of Solid Loci (Τόποι στερεοί, 5 books — lost) (apud Pappum, Synagoge VII, Hultsch II pp. 636, 670–678) [fl. c. 320 BCE]

Key: aristaeus:solid-loci Pre-Apollonian treatise on conic sections connected with the Three- and Four-Line Locus problem.

Archimedes of Syracuse — On Spirals, Book of Lemmas (Arbelos), Semi-Regular Polyhedra & Equilibria of Planes (apud Pappum, Synagoge IV pp. 208–245, V pp. 352–358, VIII p. 1060) [3rd cent. BCE]

Key: archimedes:method-spirals Primary source for Pappus’s arbelos circle chain (IV.14–18), spiral quadrature, complete table of all 13 Archimedean semi-regular solids (V.19), and center-of-gravity mechanics (VIII).

Nicomachus of Gerasa — Introduction to Arithmetic (Ἀριθμητικὴ Εἰσαγωγή, Book II) (Synagoge III, Hultsch I pp. 68–105) [c. 100 CE]

Key: nicomachus:introductio Source for the ten Pythagorean and Neo-Pythagorean medieties (μεσότητες) synthesized geometrically by Pappus in Book III.

Zenodorus — On Isoperimetric Figures (Περὶ ἰσοπεριμέτρων σχημάτων — fragments) (apud Pappum, Synagoge V, Hultsch I pp. 308–350) [2nd cent. BCE]

Key: zenodorus:isoperimetric Hellenistic treatise on planar and spatial isoperimetric inequalities reworked and sharpened by Pappus in Book V following his Preface on the Honeycomb.

Hero of Alexandria — Mechanics (Μηχανικά) & Baroulkos (Βαρουλκός) (apud Pappum, Synagoge VIII, Hultsch III pp. 1022–1073, 1104–1115) [1st cent. CE]

Key: heron:mechanica Principal Greek source for the five simple machines, toothed gear-trains, worm screw, and weight-hauling reduction preserved in Greek through Pappus Book VIII.

Proclus Diadochus — In Primum Euclidis Elementorum Librum Commentarii (ed. Friedlein 1873; geometor/proclus) [c. 470 CE]

Key: proclus:in-euclidem Companion GEOMETOR codex preserving Pappus’s symmetry proof of Elements I.5 and axiomatic remarks, while paralleling Synagoge VII on geometric analysis and synthesis.

Modern Analytic & Projective Descendants¶

René Descartes — La Géométrie (Leiden, 1637, Book I: The Pappus Locus Problem) [1637]

Key: descartes:geometrie Inaugurated analytic coordinate geometry specifically to solve the generalized $n$-line Pappus locus posed in Synagoge VII (Hultsch II, pp. 676–680) via Commandino’s 1588 Latin edition.

Blaise Pascal — Essai pour les coniques (Hexagrammum Mysticum) (Paris, February 1640) [1640]

Key: pascal:essai-coniques Generalized Pappus’s two-line Hexagon Theorem (Synagoge VII.138–139) to any smooth conic section, yielding 60 Pascal lines for six conic points.

Jakob Steiner — Systematische Entwickelung der Abhängigkeit geometrischer Gestalten von einander (Berlin: Fincke, 1832) [1832]

Key: steiner:1832 Established cross-ratio projectivity and proved that the six $S_3$ Pappus lines concur in two triples at the Steiner Star points $(S_+, S_-)$.

David Hilbert — Grundlagen der Geometrie (Leipzig: Teubner, 1899, Ch. V–VI; geometor/hilbert) [1899]

Key: hilbert:1899 Demonstrated in geometor/hilbert that Pappus’s Hexagon Theorem (Pascalischer Satz for two lines) is algebraically equivalent to commutativity of multiplication in the coordinate division ring.
