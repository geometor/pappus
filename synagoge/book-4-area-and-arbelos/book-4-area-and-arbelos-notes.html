Book IV · Philological & Mathematical Notes (Affine Area & Circle Inversion)¶

Synthetic Proofs, Inversion Duality & Cross-Corpus Commentary

1. Affine Invariance of Proposition 1 (Hultsch I, pp. 176–179)¶

Pappus concludes Proposition 1 with explicit pride: καὶ ἔστι τοῦτο καθολικώτερον πολλῷ τοῦ ἐν τοῖς ὀρθογωνίοις ἐπὶ τῶν τετραγώνων ἐν τοῖς Στοιχείοις δεδειγμένου (“and this is far more general than what is proved in the Elements on squares in right-angled triangles”). Whereas Euclid's Elements I.47 relies on the metric congruence of rotated triangles ($\triangle ABD \cong \triangle FBC$ via right-angle addition, [I.4]) and Elements VI.31 relies on proportionality of similar figures on a right triangle ([VI.8], [VI.19]), Pappus’s proof uses zero circles, zero right angles, and zero metric ratios: it belongs purely to equiaffine geometry (parallelism and area-preserving shears, [I.34]–[I.35]). Applying any invertible linear transformation $T \in \operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{R})$ to a Pythagorean windmill immediately yields a general Pappus area configuration. See also geometor/euclid (Heath 1926, Vol. I, pp. 366–367).

2. Synthetic Secant Lemmas vs. Circular Inversion in the Arbelos (Props. 14–18)¶

Modern expositions since Jakob Steiner (1826) prove Pappus’s arbelos altitude law $y_n = n\,d_n$ in three lines by circular inversion centered at the left endpoint $A = (0, 0)$ with inversion circle radius $k = 2\sqrt{R r_1}$:

  1. Because the outer semicircle $(O_0, A)$ on diameter $AC = [0, 2R]$ and the left inner semicircle $(O_1, A)$ on diameter $AD = [0, 2r_1]$ both pass through the center of inversion $A$ with vertical tangents there, inversion in $(A, k)$ maps $(O_0, A)$ and $(O_1, A)$ onto two parallel vertical straight lines $u = 2r_1$ and $u = 2R$ separated by constant strip width $2r_2$.

  2. The right inner semicircle $(O_2, D)$ maps onto the circle $C_0'$ of diameter $2r_2$ resting directly on the horizontal axis $v = 0$ between those two vertical lines, and the entire chain $C_1, C_2, \dots, C_n$ maps onto a vertical stack of congruent circles $C_1', C_2', \dots, C_n'$ of identical diameter $2r_2$, whose centers sit at heights $v_n' = 2n\,r_2 = n\,d'$.

  3. Because inversion from $A$ scales both a tangent circle’s diameter and its center’s perpendicular height above $AC$ by the same factor (up to a classical secant-tangent power correction proved synthetically in Pappus’s Lemmas 15–17 via [III.36]), the integer ratio $y_n / d_n = v_n' / d' = n$ is strictly invariant!

What makes Pappus’s synthetic proof in Props. 15–18 (Hultsch I pp. 210–228) historically remarkable is that Lemma 15 (collinearity of the radical center $Z$ with external/internal contact points $E, P$ and equality $ZE \cdot ZP = Z\Theta^2$) is literally circular inversion executed in pure Euclidean ratio language fifteen centuries before Apollonian/Steinerian inversion was named—compare geometor/altshiller-court (Chapter IX, Inversion) and Ptolemy's Planisphaerium §4 in geometor/ptolemy.

3. Notice on $C_2$ and $C_3$ Sharing Equal Height at $(r_1, r_2) = (2, 1)$¶

In the canonical $(r_1, r_2) = (2, 1)$ preset rendered in pappus_arbelos_chain.svg, notice the delightful arithmetic symmetry at $n = 2$ and $n = 3$:

  • At $n = 2$: $\Delta_2 = 6 + 4 = 10$, so $d_2 = \frac{12}{10} = \frac{6}{5}$ and $y_2 = 2\,d_2 = \frac{12}{5}$.

  • At $n = 3$: $\Delta_3 = 6 + 9 = 15$, so $d_3 = \frac{12}{15} = \frac{4}{5}$ and $y_3 = 3\,d_3 = \frac{12}{5}$.

Why do $K_2 = \left(3, \frac{12}{5}\right)$ and $K_3 = \left(2, \frac{12}{5}\right)$ sit at the exact same maximum altitude $y = \frac{12}{5}$? Because all centers $K_n$ lie on the ellipse with foci $O_1 = (2, 0)$ and $O_0 = (3, 0)$, centered at $x = \frac{5}{2}$ with semi-minor axis $b = \sqrt{r_1 R} = \sqrt{6} \approx 2.4495$, and $K_2$ ($x = 3$) and $K_3$ ($x = 2$) straddle the ellipse’s apex $x = \frac{5}{2}$ symmetrically!
