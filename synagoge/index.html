Synagoge (Μαθηματικὴ Συναγωγή) — Multi-Track Book Codex¶

Pappus of Alexandria (fl. c. 320–340 CE) · Critical Greek Text (Friedrich Hultsch, 1876–1878) · Original GEOMETOR English Translation · Exact SymPy / geometor.model Demonstrations

Around 340 CE in Alexandria, Pappus gathered seven centuries of classical and Hellenistic geometry into his eight-book Synagoge (Mathematical Collection). Far more than an encyclopedic compiler, Pappus repaired lost demonstrations, classified geometric problems into plane, solid, and linear loci, generalized Euclid's Pythagorean area theorem to arbitrary parallelograms, proved the invariant altitude law of the arbelos tangent circle chain, demonstrated the isoperimetric optimality of the honeycomb, preserved the 33-book Treasury of Analysis alongside the Pappus–Guldinus Centroid Rule, and established the Cross-Ratio and Hexagon Collinearity Theorems that birthed modern projective geometry.

Each treatise unit in this Multi-Track Book Codex synchronizes four parallel tracks:

  1. English (-en) — Fresh, complete scholarly translation authored directly from Hultsch's 1876–1878 critical Greek text, wired with interactive :g: vector diagrams.

  2. Greek (Hultsch 1876) (-el) — Diplomatic polytonic Greek text from Weidmann Vols. I–III (1876, 1877, 1878), keyed by Hultsch page and proposition numbers and wired to the same interactive SVGs.

  3. Summary (-summary) — Concise structural argument map, exact algebraic invariants, and geometor.pappus construction audit.

  4. Notes (-notes) — Philological commentary, Commandino (1588) and Heath (1921) reception notes, and cross-corpus links to euclid, elements, apollonius, archimedes, ptolemy, proclus, and hilbert.
