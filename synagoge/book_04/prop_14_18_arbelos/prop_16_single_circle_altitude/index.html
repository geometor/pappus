Proposition 16: Altitude Relation for an Inscribed Circle¶

Synagoge Book IV, Chs. 23–24 / Prop. 16 (Hultsch I, pp. 212–218) · Original English Translation

23. (16) Let there be two semicircles \(BHG\) and \(BE\Delta\), and a circle \(EZH\Theta\) tangent to them, and from its center \(A\) let the perpendicular \(AM\) be drawn to the base \(B\Gamma\) of the semicircles; to show that as \(MB\) is to the radius of circle

\(EZH\Theta\), so in the first figure the sum of \(\Gamma B\) and \(B\Delta\) is to their difference \(\Gamma\Delta\), while in the second and third figures the difference of \(\Gamma B\) and \(B\Delta\) is to their sum, that is, to \(\Gamma\Delta\).
step Through \(A\) let \(\Theta Z\) be drawn parallel to \(B\Gamma\). Since then two circles \(BH\Gamma\) and \(EZH\Theta\) touch one another at \(H\), and diameters \(B\Gamma\) and \(Z\Theta\) in them are parallel, both the line through \(H, \Theta, B\) and that through \(H, Z, \Gamma\) will be straight. Again, since two circles \(BE\Delta\) and \(EZH\Theta\) touch one another at \(E\), and parallel diameters \(\Theta Z\) and \(B\Delta\) are in them, both the line through \(Z, E, B\) and that through \(\Theta, E, \Delta\) will be straight. Let perpendiculars \(\Theta K\) and \(Z\Lambda\) also be drawn from points \(\Theta\) and \(Z\). Then by the similarity of triangles \(BH\Gamma\) and \(B\Theta K\), as \(B\Gamma\) is to \(BH\), so \(B\Theta\) is to \(BK\), and the rectangle contained by \(\Gamma B\) and \(BK\) equals that contained by \(HB\) and \(B\Theta\); and by the similarity of triangles \(BZ\Lambda\) and \(BE\Delta\), as \(\Delta B\) is to \(BE\), so \(BZ\) is to \(B\Lambda\), and rectangle \(\Delta B\cdot B\Lambda\) equals rectangle \(ZB\cdot BE\). And rectangle \(HB\cdot B\Theta\) equals rectangle \(ZB\cdot BE\); therefore rectangle \(\Gamma B\cdot BK\) also equals rectangle \(\Delta B\cdot B\Lambda\), or if the perpendicular from \(Z\) falls upon \(\Delta\),

step the square on \(B\Delta\) ([III.36]). Thus in the first figure as \(\Gamma B\) is to \(B\Delta\), so \(\Lambda B\) is to \(BK\), so that the sum of \(\Gamma B\) and \(B\Delta\) is to their difference \(\Gamma\Delta\) as the sum of \(\Lambda B\) and \(BK\) is to their difference \(K\Lambda\). And \(BM\) is half of the sum of \(\Lambda B\) and \(BK\) (since \(KM\) equals \(M\Lambda\)), while \(MK\) is half of \(\Lambda K\); therefore as the sum of \(\Gamma B\) and \(B\Delta\) is to \(\Gamma\Delta\), so \(BM\) is to \(MK\), that is, to the radius of circle \(EZH\Theta\). In the second and third figures, since rectangle \(\Gamma BK\) was shown equal to rectangle \(\Delta B\Lambda\), as \(\Gamma B\) is to \(B\Delta\), so \(\Lambda B\) is to \(BK\). By composition, as \(\Gamma\Delta\) is to \(\Delta B\), so \(K\Lambda\) is to \(KB\); hence as \(\Gamma\Delta\) is to the difference of \(\Gamma B\) and \(B\Delta\), so \(K\Lambda\) is to the difference of \(\Lambda B\) and \(BK\). And half of \(K\Lambda\) is the radius of circle \(EZH\Theta\), while \(BM\) is half of the difference of \(\Lambda B\) and \(BK\) because \(\Lambda M\) equals \(MK\); so that as \(MB\) is to the radius of circle \(EZH\Theta\), so in the first figure the sum of \(\Gamma B\) and \(B\Delta\) is to their difference \(\Gamma\Delta\), and in the second and third figures the difference of \(\Gamma B\) and \(B\Delta\) is to their sum, that is, to \(\Gamma\Delta\).

step 24. It is observed at the same time that the rectangle contained by \(BK\) and \(\Lambda\Gamma\) equals the square on \(AM\). For by the similarity of triangles \(B\Theta K\) and \(Z\Lambda\Gamma\), as \(BK\) is to \(K\Theta\), so \(Z\Lambda\) is to \(\Lambda\Gamma\), and rectangle \(BK\cdot\Lambda\Gamma\) equals rectangle \(\Theta K\cdot Z\Lambda\), that is, the square on \(AM\). And because as \(B\Gamma\) is to \(\Gamma\Delta\), so \(B\Lambda\) is to \(K\Lambda\), the rectangle contained by \(B\Gamma\) and \(K\Lambda\), that is, the diameter of the circle, equals rectangle \(B\Lambda\cdot\Delta\Gamma\); and because as \(B\Delta\) is to \(\Gamma\Delta\), so \(BK\) is to \(K\Lambda\), the rectangle contained by \(B\Delta\) and \(K\Lambda\), that is, the diameter of the circle, equals rectangle \(BK\cdot\Delta\Gamma\).
