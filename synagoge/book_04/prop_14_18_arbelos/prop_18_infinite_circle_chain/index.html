Proposition 18: Induction for the Infinite Circle Chain¶

Synagoge Book IV, Chs. 26–27 / Prop. 18 (Hultsch I, pp. 224–230) · Original English Translation

26. (18) These things having been proved beforehand, let semicircle BHΓ be supposed, and upon its base let any point Δ be taken, and upon \(B\Delta\) and \(\Delta\Gamma\) let semicircles BEΔ and ΔYΓ be described, and into the space between the three circumferences, called the arbelos, let circles be inscribed tangent to the semicircles and to one another, as many as one may please, such as those about centers A, Π, O, and from their centers let perpendiculars AM, ΠN, OΣ be drawn upon \(B\Gamma\); I say that AM equals the diameter of the circle about \(A\), ΠN is double the diameter of the circle about \(\Pi\), OΣ is triple the diameter of the circle about \(O\), and the succeeding perpendiculars are multiples of their own diameters according to the successive numbers exceeding one another by unity.

And on this account as \(BK\) is to \(K\Lambda\), so \(K\Lambda\) is to \(\Lambda\Gamma\); for each ratio is the same as that of \(B\Delta\) to \(\Delta\Gamma\) (for since rectangle \(\Gamma B\cdot BK\) equals rectangle \(\Lambda B\cdot B\Delta\), therefore as \(\Gamma B\) is to \(B\Lambda\), so \(\Delta B\) is to \(BK\); by division as \(\Gamma\Delta\) is to \(\Delta B\), so \(\Lambda K\) is to \(KB\); by inversion as \(B\Delta\) is to \(\Delta\Gamma\), so \(BK\) is to \(K\Lambda\); again since rectangle \(B\Gamma\cdot \Gamma\Lambda\) equals rectangle \(K\Gamma\cdot\Gamma\Delta\), therefore as \(B\Gamma\) is to \(\Gamma K\), so \(\Gamma\Delta\) is to \(\Gamma\Lambda\); alternately as \(B\Gamma\) is to \(\Gamma\Delta\), so \(K\Gamma\) is to \(\Gamma\Lambda\); by division therefore as \(B\Delta\) is to \(\Delta\Gamma\), so \(K\Lambda\) is to \(\Lambda\Gamma\); and as \(B\Delta\) is to \(\Gamma\Delta\), so also was \(BK\) to \(K\Lambda\); therefore as \(BK\) is to \(K\Lambda\), so \(K\Lambda\) is to \(\Lambda\Gamma\)). Therefore rectangle \(BK\cdot\Lambda\Gamma\) equals the square on \(K\Lambda\). But rectangle \(BK\cdot\Lambda\Gamma\) was also shown equal to the square on \(AM\); therefore \(AM\) equals \(K\Lambda\), that is, diameter \(Z\Theta\) of the circle about \(A\). And since this too

Therefore \(\Pi N\) together with the diameter of the circle about \(\Pi\) is triple the diameter, and \(O\Sigma\) is in the same ratio to the diameter of the circle about \(O\); therefore \(O\Sigma\) is triple the diameter of the circle about \(O\). And likewise the perpendicular of the next circle is quadruple its diameter, and the succeeding perpendiculars will be found to be multiples of their respective diameters according to the successive numbers exceeding one another by unity, and this will be demonstrated as occurring without end.

For in general whatever ratio \(B\Gamma\) has to \(\Gamma\Delta\), that ratio in power does \(\Delta Z\) have to the diameter of the circle about \(A\), as is shown next. For example, if \(B\Gamma\) be quadruple \(\Gamma\Delta\) in length, \(\Delta Z\), that is, the perpendicular from \(A\), becomes double in length the diameter of the circle about \(A\), and that from \(\Pi\) triple, and that from \(O\) quadruple, and so on according to the successive numbers.
