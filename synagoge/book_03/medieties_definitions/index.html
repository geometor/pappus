Critique of the Semicircle Claim and Definitions of the Three Medieties¶

Synagoge Book III, Chapters 28–30 (Hultsch I, pp. 68–72) · Original English Translation

28. Now the second of the problems was this. Someone else claimed to take the three medieties in a semicircle, and setting out semicircle \(AB\Gamma\), whose center is \(E\), and taking any point \(\Delta\) on \(A\Gamma\), and drawing \(\Delta B\) at right angles to \(E\Gamma\) from it, and joining \(EB\), and drawing \(\Delta Z\) perpendicular to it from \(\Delta\), he asserted simply that the three medieties were set forth in the semicircle: \(E\Gamma\) the arithmetic mean, \(\Delta B\) the geometric mean, and \(BZ\) the harmonic.

That \(B\Delta\) is a mean between \(A\Delta\), \(\Delta\Gamma\) in geometric proportion, and \(E\Gamma\) between \(A\Delta\), \(\Delta\Gamma\) in arithmetic mediety, is manifest; for \(A\Delta : \Delta B = \Delta B : \Delta\Gamma\), while \(A\Delta : A\Delta\) equals the excess of \(A\Delta, AE\), that is of \(A\Delta, E\Gamma\), to the excess of \(E\Gamma, \Gamma\Delta\). But how \(ZB\) is a mean of the harmonic

mediety, or of what straight lines, he did not say, only that it is third proportional to \(EB, B\Delta\), being ignorant that from \(EB, B\Delta, BZ\) being in geometric proportion the harmonic mediety is framed; for it will be shown by us later that two \(EB\), three \(\Delta B\), and one \(BZ\) placed together as one make the greater extreme of the harmonic mediety, two \(B\Delta\) and one \(BZ\) the mean, and one \(B\Delta\) and one \(BZ\) the least.

29. First we must treat of the three medieties [and after these of those in a semicircle], then of the other three opposed to them according to the ancients, and later of the four among the moderns according to their opinions, and how it is possible to find each of the ten medieties through geometric proportion, so that we may confirm the proposed refutation through multiple proofs.

On the Three Medieties.

30. A mediety differs from a proportion in this, that if anything is a proportion, it is also a mediety, yet not conversely. For there are three medieties, of which one is arithmetic, another geometric, and another harmonic.

A mediety is called arithmetic when, of three terms, the middle exceeds one of the extremes by the same as it is exceeded by the other (as \(6\) stands to \(9\) and \(3\)), or when the first term is to itself as the first excess is to the second. [One must understand the greater terms first.]

A mediety is called geometric, that is, proportion properly so called, when the middle term is to one of the extremes as the remaining extreme is to the middle (as the number \(6\) stands to \(12\) and \(3\)), and otherwise when the first term is to the second as the first excess is to the second.

f. 0105 · p. 72

A harmonic mediety is when the middle term exceeds one of the extremes by the same fraction of itself by which it is exceeded by the other (as \(3\) stands to \(2\) and \(6\)), or when the first term is to the third as the first excess is to the second.

These things being supposed, we shall find together the three medieties in least straight lines, five in number, the following having been premised.
