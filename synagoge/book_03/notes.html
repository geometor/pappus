Book III · Philological & Mathematical Notes¶

Textual Transmission, Reception & Cross-Corpus Commentary

1. Pandrosion and Fourth-Century Alexandrian Pedagogy (Hultsch I, p. 30, l. 3)¶

In Codex Vaticanus Graecus 218 (the 10th-century archetype from which all extant manuscripts of the Synagoge descend), the opening vocative of Book III reads ὦ κρατίστη Πανδροσίων (“O most noble Pandrosion”). In his 1588 Latin editio princeps, Federico Commandino emended the feminine superlative κρατίστη to masculine, assuming the addressee was a male teacher. Friedrich Hultsch (1876, Vol. I, p. 30 apparatus) restored the manuscript’s feminine form, establishing Pandrosion of Alexandria as one of the earliest documented women mathematicians in antiquity—nearly two generations before Hypatia (c. 355–415 CE).

2. Two Versions of the Semicircle Mean Construction (Props. 28–30 vs. Prop. 16)¶

As Heath (1921, Vol. II, pp. 367–369) and geometor/euclid (Heath 1926, Vol. II, p. 211, note on Elements VI.13) observe, Book III contains two semicircular mean constructions:

  1. Pappus’s Canonical Three-in-One Semicircle (Chs. 40–43, Hultsch I pp. 80–84, modeled in construct_three_means()): Given $AD = a$ and $DC = b$ contiguous along diameter $AC$, a single perpendicular $DB$ and a single altitude $DF \perp OB$ exhibit $A = OB$, $G = DB$, and $H = BF$ simultaneously on one diagram.

  2. The Anonymous Craftsman’s Tangential Variant (Ch. 39, Hultsch I pp. 70–72): A geometer (τις) proposed exhibiting the three means using an external secant and tangent, which Pappus critiques because the presenter omitted the proof of $(a - H) : (H - b) = a : b$.

3. Coordinate Check on the External Harmonic Conjugate $T$¶

With $A = (0, 0)$, $D = (a, 0)$, and $C = (a + b, 0)$ ($a < b$), center $O = \left(\frac{a+b}{2}, 0\right)$ and $B = (a, \sqrt{ab})$. Since $OB$ has vector $\left(\frac{a - b}{2}, \sqrt{ab}\right)$, the circle tangent at $B$ perpendicular to $OB$ meets $y = 0$ at $x_T = -\frac{a(a + b)}{b - a}$. For $(a, b) = (9, 16)$, $x_T = -\frac{225}{7}$, yielding $\operatorname{CR}(A, C; D, T) = -1$.

4. Cross-References in the GEOMETOR Canon¶

  • Euclid’s Elements (geometor/euclid & geometor/elements): Elements II.5, III.31, VI.8, VI.13, and XIII.13–18 (re-inscribed analytically in Book III Chs. 54–58).

  • Golden Ratio Engine (geometor/divine): Setting $(a, b) = (1, \phi^2)$ embeds $G = \phi$ cleanly inside $\mathbb{Q}(\phi)$.
