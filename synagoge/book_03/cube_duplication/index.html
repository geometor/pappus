Delian Cube Duplication and Two Mean Proportionals¶

Synagoge Book III, Chs. 22–27 / Props. 1–5 (Hultsch I, pp. 56–68; App. IV, p. 1217) · Original English Translation

Because conic sections are delicate to draw directly in the plane by hand, the ancients also devised exact mechanical and neusis constructions for inserting two mean proportionals ($a : x = x : y = y : b$) between two given straight lines (Chs. 22–27, Props. 1–5, Hultsch I pp. 56–68):

  1. The Mesolabe of Eratosthenes (Prop. 1): Three sliding rectangular or trapezoidal congruent triangles shifted along parallel rulers until intersecting vertices align collinearly on a common transversal ray.

  2. The Conchoidal Verging Ruler of Nicomedes (Prop. 2): An auxiliary triangle extended by a marked rule pivoted through a fixed pole so that the intercept cut off between an asymptote and a given line equals a prescribed length (neusis).

  3. The Rectangle-and-Pivot Method of Hero of Alexandria (Prop. 3): A graduated straightedge pivoted around the corner of a rectangle $a \times b$ until equal segments fall on the extended adjacent sides.

  4. Pappus’s Own Semicircular Construction (*Prop. 4–5*): Turning a straightedge around one endpoint of a semicircle until its chord and interior prolongation stand in the desired proportion—equivalent in geometric effect to Hero’s method while adapting seamlessly to planar surveying instruments.
