Book III · Problem Hierarchy & The Three Means¶

Μαθηματικὴ Συναγωγή Γ΄ — Pappus of Alexandria, ed. Friedrich Hultsch (Vol. I, pp. 30–84)

Three Classical Means in a Single Semicircle

Let two unequal straight segments AD $= a$ and DC $= b$ be placed end-to-end along diameter AC of semicircle ABC with center O. Erect DB perpendicular to AC meeting the circumference at B, join radius OB, and drop DF perpendicular to OB at F. Then radius OB is the arithmetic mean $A$, ordinate DB is the geometric mean $G$, and segment BF cut off from the vertex B is the harmonic mean $H$ of $a$ and $b$; moreover, the tangent BT at B meets diameter AC produced at the external harmonic conjugate T satisfying $(A, C; D, T) = -1$.

Golden-Section Specialization in Q(phi)

Algebraic closure and geometric properties of Pappus’s semicircle means when diameter segments stand in the ratio of the square of the golden ratio (a = 1, b = phi^2).

Book III · Philological & Mathematical Notes

In Codex Vaticanus Graecus 218 (the 10th-century archetype from which all extant manuscripts of the Synagoge descend), the opening vocative of Book III reads ὦ κρατίστη Πανδροσίων (“O most noble Pandrosion”). In his 1588 Latin editio princeps, Federico Commandino emended the feminine superlative κρατίστη to masculine, assuming the addressee was a male teacher. Friedrich Hultsch (1876, Vol. I, p. 30 apparatus) restored the manuscript’s feminine form, establishing Pandrosion of Alexandria as one of the earliest documented women mathematicians in antiquity—nearly two generations before Hypatia (c. 355–415 CE).
