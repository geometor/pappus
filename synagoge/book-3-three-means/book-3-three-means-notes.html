Book III · Philological & Mathematical Notes (Pandrosion & Medieties)¶

Textual Transmission, Reception & Cross-Corpus Commentary

1. Pandrosion and Fourth-Century Alexandrian Pedagogy (Hultsch I, p. 30, l. 3)¶

In Codex Vaticanus Graecus 218 (the 10th-century archetype from which all extant manuscripts of the Synagoge descend), the opening vocative of Book III reads ὦ κρατίστη Πανδροσίων (“O most noble Pandrosion”). In his 1588 Latin editio princeps, Federico Commandino emended the feminine superlative κρατίστη to masculine, assuming the addressee was a male teacher. Friedrich Hultsch (1876, Vol. I, p. 30 apparatus) restored the manuscript’s feminine form, establishing Pandrosion of Alexandria as one of the earliest documented women mathematicians in antiquity—nearly two generations before Hypatia (c. 355–415 CE). While Pappus polemically critiques a student of Pandrosion’s circle who mistook the Delian cube-duplication problem for a plane locus, the mathematical sophistication of Book III (covering solid neuseis, all ten Neo-Pythagorean medieties, paradoxical inscribed triangles, and an alternative analytical inscription of the five Platonic solids in Chs. 54–58) testifies to an active competitive school of higher geometry in early fourth-century Alexandria.

2. Two Versions of the Semicircle Mean Construction (Props. 28–30 vs. Prop. 16)¶

As Heath (1921, Vol. II, pp. 367–369) and geometor/euclid (Heath 1926, Vol. II, p. 211, note on Elements VI.13) observe, Book III contains two semicircular mean constructions:

  1. Pappus’s Canonical Three-in-One Semicircle (Chs. 40–43, Hultsch I pp. 80–84, modeled in construct_three_means()): Given $AD = a$ and $DC = b$ contiguous along diameter $AC$, a single perpendicular $DB$ and a single altitude $DF \perp OB$ exhibit $A = OB$, $G = DB$, and $H = BF$ simultaneously on one diagram, yielding a visual proof without words of both $A \cdot H = G^2$ and $A \ge G \ge H$.

  2. The Anonymous Craftsman’s Tangential Variant (Ch. 39, Hultsch I pp. 70–72): A clever geometer (τις) proposed exhibiting the three means using an external secant and tangent, which Pappus critiques because the presenter omitted the proof that the third segment actually satisfies the harmonic definition $(a - H) : (H - b) = a : b$—a gap Pappus supplies before presenting his cleaner internal construction.

3. Coordinate Check on the External Harmonic Conjugate $T$¶

Wait—notice how Book III’s metric harmonic mean $H = \frac{2ab}{a+b}$ connects directly to Book VII’s projective cross-ratio! With $A = (0, 0)$, $D = (a, 0)$, and $C = (a + b, 0)$ ($a < b$), center $O = \left(\frac{a+b}{2}, 0\right)$ and $B = (a, \sqrt{ab})$. Since $OB$ has vector $\left(\frac{a - b}{2}, \sqrt{ab}\right)$, the circle tangent at $B$ perpendicular to $OB$ meets the diameter line $y = 0$ at

\[x_T = a - \frac{2ab}{b - a} = -\frac{a(a + b)}{b - a}.\]

For the rational Pythagorean preset $(a, b) = (9, 16)$, $x_T = -\frac{9 \cdot 25}{7} = -\frac{225}{7}$. Computing the collinear cross-ratio of $(A, C; D, T)$ along $y = 0$:

\[(A, C; D, T) = \frac{(0 - 9)\left(25 - \left(-\frac{225}{7}\right)\right)}{\left(0 - \left(-\frac{225}{7}\right)\right)(25 - 9)} = \frac{-9 \cdot \frac{400}{7}}{\frac{225}{7} \cdot 16} = \frac{-3600}{3600} = -1.\]
