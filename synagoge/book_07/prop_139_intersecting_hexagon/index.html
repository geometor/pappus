Proposition 139: Pappus’s General Hexagon Collinearity Theorem¶

Synagoge Book VII, Proposition 139 / Ch. 207 (Hultsch II, p. 886, Lemma XIII to Euclid’s Porisms) · Original English Translation

207 (Lemma XIII, Prop. 139). But let \(AB, \Gamma\Delta\) not be parallel, and let them meet at \(N\); to show again that the line through \(H, M, K\) is straight.

Since into three straight lines \(AN, AZ, A\Delta\) from the same point \(\Gamma\) two straight lines \(\Gamma E, \Gamma\Delta\) have been drawn through, it comes to be as the rectangle \(\Gamma E, H\Theta\) to \(\Gamma H, \Theta E\), so the rectangle \(\Gamma N, Z\Delta\) to \(N\Delta, \Gamma Z\). Again, since from the same point \(\Delta\) into three straight lines \(BN, B\Gamma, BZ\) two straight lines \(\Delta E, \Delta N\) have been drawn through, as the rectangle \(N\Gamma, Z\Delta\) is to \(N\Delta, Z\Gamma\), so is \(\Delta K, E\Lambda\) to \(\Delta E, K\Lambda\). Yet as \(N\Gamma, Z\Delta\) is to \(N\Delta, \Gamma Z\), so was shown to be \(\Gamma E, H\Theta\) to \(\Gamma H, \Theta E\); and therefore as \(\Gamma E, \Theta H\) is to \(\Gamma H, \Theta E\), so is \(\Delta K, E\Lambda\) to \(\Delta E, K\Lambda\) [it has been reduced to what also holds for parallels]. By what was written before, therefore, the line through \(H, M, K\) is straight.
