Proposition 129: Cross-Ratio Invariance Across Central Projection

Proposition 129 (Lemma III to Euclid’s Porisms): If from a single point $O$ four straight rays are drawn, and two transversals fall across them cutting them in points $A, B, C, D$ and $A', B', C', D'$, then the compound ratio of the whole segment and the middle segment to the extremes on the first line equals the compound ratio on the second line:

\[\frac{AC \cdot BD}{AD \cdot BC} = \frac{A'C' \cdot B'D'}{A'D' \cdot B'C'} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \operatorname{CR}(A, B; C, D) = \operatorname{CR}(A', B'; C', D').\]

Proposition 131: Complete-Quadrilateral Harmonic Conjugates

Proposition 131 (Harmonic Division on a Complete Quadrilateral): Given three collinear points $A, B, C$, to find the fourth point $D$ on their line such that $(A, B; C, D)$ is divided harmonically—that is:

\[\frac{AC}{CB} = \frac{AD}{DB} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \operatorname{CR}(A, B; C, D) = -1.\]
Greek (Hultsch 1876) · English · Summary · Notes

Proposition 138: Pappus Hexagon on Parallel Carriers

Proposition 138 (Pappus Hexagon on Parallel Lines): If two straight lines A0A2 and B0B2 are parallel, and upon them points A0, A1, A2 and B0, B1, B2 be taken, and their cross-joins A0B1 & A1B0, A0B2 & A2B0, A1B2 & A2B1 meet in points M01, M20, and M12, then these three points lie on a single straight line parallel to the original carrier lines.

Book VII · Philological & Mathematical Notes

Of all the lost works of antiquity, Euclid’s three books of Porisms (Πορίσματα) have stimulated the greatest mathematical detective work (Fermat, Halley, Simson, Chasles). Book VII of the Synagoge is our primary surviving source, preserving 38 preparatory lemmas.
