Proposition 131: Complete-Quadrilateral Harmonic Conjugates¶

Synagoge Book VII, Proposition 131 (Hultsch II, pp. 874–876, Lemma V to Euclid’s Porisms; App. IV, pp. 1264, 1269)

In Proposition 131, Pappus applies cross-ratio invariance to the geometry of the complete quadrilateral, demonstrating that opposite sides and diagonals cut any transversal line across the figure in harmonic division ($\operatorname{CR} = -1$).

Proposition 131 (Harmonic Division on a Complete Quadrilateral): Given three collinear points $A, B, C$, to find the fourth point $D$ on their line such that $(A, B; C, D)$ is divided harmonically—that is:

\[\frac{AC}{CB} = \frac{AD}{DB} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \operatorname{CR}(A, B; C, D) = -1.\]
step Construction (Straightedge Alone): Take any point $P$ not on line $AB$, and draw rays $PA, PB, PC$. From any point $Q$ on $PB$, draw a line through $C$ meeting $PA$ at $R$. Let $AQ$ and $BR$ intersect at $S$. The join $PS$ meets line $AB$ at the fourth harmonic point $D$.
step Demonstration: The four vertices $P, Q, R, S$ form a complete quadrilateral whose three pairs of opposite sides are $(PQ, RS)$, $(PR, QS)$, and $(PS, QR)$. Central projection from $P$ onto $QR$ followed by projection from $A$ back onto line $AB$ preserves the cross-ratio, forcing $\operatorname{CR}(A, B; C, D) = -1$.
