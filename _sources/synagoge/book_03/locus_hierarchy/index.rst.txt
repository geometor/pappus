The Threefold Hierarchy of Loci: Plane, Solid, and Linear
=========================================================

.. rubric:: *Synagoge* Book III, Chapters 20–21 (:cite:`Hultsch I, pp. 54–56 <hultsch-1876>`) · Original English Translation

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0087
   :page: 54

**20.** The ancients say that there are **three genera** of :term:`problems in geometry <topos-epipedos-stereos-grammikos>`, and that some of them are called **plane**, others **solid**, and others **linear**.
Those which can be solved by means of a straight line and a circle's circumference are reasonably called **plane** (*ἐπίπεδα*), for the lines through which such problems are solved have their generation in a plane. All problems solved by taking one or more of the sections of the cone into the discovery are called **solid** (*στερεά*),
for to the construction it is necessary to make use of surfaces of solid figures, I mean conic surfaces. A third genus still remains, which is called **linear** (*γραμμικόν*): for other lines besides those mentioned are taken for the construction, having a more varied and constrained generation, such as spirals, quadratrices, conchoids, and cissoids, possessing many paradoxical properties.

**21.** Such then being the difference among problems, the ancient geometers were not able to construct the aforementioned problem upon the two straight lines—which is by nature solid—following the purely geometrical method, since it was not easy to draw the sections of the cone in a plane,
and transferring it to instruments they brought it wonderfully into manual practice and construction, as may be seen in the mesolabe of :person:`Eratosthenes <eratosthenes>`

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0089
   :page: 56

and the mechanics [or catapult treatises] of Philo and :person:`Hero <heron>`. For these, acknowledging the problem to be solid, executed its construction instrumentally alone
[in agreement with :person:`Apollonius of Perga <apollonius>`, who also made its :term:`analysis <analysis>` through the sections of the cone, and others through the solid loci of :person:`Aristaeus <aristaeus>`, but none through the curves properly called plane], whereas :person:`Nicomedes <nicomedes>` solved it by the conchoid line, through which he also trisected the angle.
