The Three-, Four-, and Multi-Line Locus Problem
===============================================

.. rubric:: *Synagoge* Book VII, Chs. 36–39 (:cite:`Hultsch II, pp. 676–680 <hultsch:1877>`; App. IV, p. 1258) · Original English Translation

.. folio:: 0270
   :page: 1258

In reviewing :cite:`Apollonius's Conics <apollonius:conics>`, Pappus records the celebrated **Three- and Four-Line Locus** (*ὁ ἐπὶ τρεῖς καὶ τέσσαρας γραμμὰς τόπος*, *pp. 676–680*): if straight lines are drawn from a point at given angles to three (or four) lines given in position, and the rectangle contained by two of them bears a given ratio to the square on the third (or to the rectangle contained by the remaining two), the locus of the point is a **conic section**.

When generalized to five, six, or arbitrarily many $n$ lines using **compound ratios** (*λόγοι συγκείμενοι*)—so that no impossible dimension beyond the cube or solid parallelepiped need be invoked—the ancients left the higher locus unconstructed, bequeathing the exact challenge through which :person:`René Descartes <descartes>` launched analytic geometry in :cite:`La Géométrie (1637) <descartes:geometrie>`.
