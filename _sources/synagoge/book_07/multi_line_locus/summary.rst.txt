Summary: Multi-Line Locus & Pappus–Guldinus Theorem
===================================================

.. rubric:: Structural Argument Map & Modern Mathematical Formulation

1. The Three- and Four-Line Loci to Conics
------------------------------------------

* **3-Line Locus**: A point $P$ moves such that the distance $d_1$ to line $L_1$ and $d_2$ to line $L_2$ satisfy $d_1 \cdot d_2 = k \cdot d_3^2$ (or in given directions). The locus of $P$ is a **conic section**.
* **4-Line Locus**: $d_1 \cdot d_2 = k \cdot d_3 \cdot d_4$. The locus is likewise a **conic section**.
* **General $n$-Line Locus**: For $2k$ lines, $\prod_{i=1}^k d_i = C \prod_{j=k+1}^{2k} d_j$. For $2k-1$ lines, a segment of given length is introduced to preserve dimensionality via **compound ratios** (*λόγοι συγκείμενοι*).

2. Pappus–Guldinus Centroid Theorem
-----------------------------------

For any plane figure of area $A$ (or plane curve of arc length $L$) revolved completely through $2\pi$ about an axis in its plane not intersecting the figure:

.. math::

   V = 2\pi \bar{r} A, \qquad S = 2\pi \bar{r} L

where $\bar{r}$ is the perpendicular distance from the :term:`center of gravity <kentron-barous>` of the figure (or curve) to the axis of revolution.
In Pappus's compound ratio language:

.. math::

   \frac{V_1}{V_2} = \left(\frac{A_1}{A_2}\right) \cdot \left(\frac{\bar{r}_1}{\bar{r}_2}\right)
