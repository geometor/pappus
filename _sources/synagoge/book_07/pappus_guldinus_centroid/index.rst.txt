Pappus–Guldinus Centroid Theorem for Solids of Revolution
=========================================================

.. rubric:: *Synagoge* Book VII, Chs. 41–42 (:cite:`Hultsch II, pp. 680–682 <hultsch-1877>`) · Original English Translation

.. folio:: hultsch_1877_vol2:0225
   :page: 680

**41.** [Those who

.. folio:: hultsch_1877_vol2:0227
   :page: 682

look upon these things are in no way puffed up, as were each of the ancients and those who wrote better things; yet I, seeing everyone busied with the rudiments of mathematics and with the matter of inquiries set before them by nature,
feeling ashamed when I have shown things far superior and bringing much benefit, in order that I may not **42.** depart from the discourse having uttered this with empty hands, will give these things to my readers:

The ratio of complete solids of revolution is compounded of the ratio of the revolving figures and of the straight lines similarly drawn to the axes
from the :term:`centers of gravity <kentron-barous>` within them; while the ratio of incomplete solids of revolution is compounded of the ratio of the revolving figures and of the circumferences described by the centers of gravity within them; and the ratio of these circumferences is evidently compounded of the ratio of the drawn lines
and of the angles contained by their extremities, should they be at the axes of revolution. And these propositions, being almost a single one, embrace very many and varied theorems of lines, surfaces, and solids, all at once and by a single proof—both things not yet demonstrated and things already demonstrated, such as those in the twelfth book of these *Elements*.]

Now the eight books of :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>`'s :cite:`Conics <apollonius-conics>` contain 487 theorems or diagrams, and 70 [that is, taken into them] lemmas.
