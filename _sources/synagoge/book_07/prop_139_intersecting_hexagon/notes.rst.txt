1. Blaise Pascal and the Conic Generalization (1640)
----------------------------------------------------

In his 1640 broadside :cite:`Essay pour les coniques <pascal-essai-coniques>`, :person:`Blaise Pascal <pascal>` generalized Pappus's two-line pair to any smooth conic (*Hexagrammum Mysticum*).

2. David Hilbert and Field Commutativity (1899)
-----------------------------------------------

In :cite:`Grundlagen der Geometrie <hilbert-1899>`, :person:`David Hilbert <hilbert>` proved that Pappus VII.139 (Hultsch Ch. 207, :cite:`Hultsch II, p. 886 <hultsch-1877>`, Greek folio ``hultsch_1877_vol2:0432``; Lemma XIII to :cite:`Euclid's Porisms <euclid-porisms>`) holds in a Desarguesian projective plane if and only if multiplication in the coordinate skew-field is commutative (:math:`a \cdot b = b \cdot a`).

In the Greek manuscript tradition (*Codex Vaticanus Graecus 218*), :person:`Commandino <commandino>` (:cite:`1588 <commandino-1588>`) restored the intersection letter :math:`N` (where the manuscripts read :math:`H`) and the vertex :math:`\Gamma` and line :math:`\Gamma\Delta` in lines 7–8; :person:`Hultsch <hultsch>` also brackets the retrospective scholium at lines 20–21. Plates ``0415.g1`` and ``0415.g2`` illustrate the Porisms quadrilateral taxonomy (*hyption* and *paryption*) from Hultsch Vol. III's *Index Graecitatis*.
