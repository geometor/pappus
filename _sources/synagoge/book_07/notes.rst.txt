Book VII · Philological & Mathematical Notes (*Porisms & Projective Geometry*)
=====================================================================================

.. rubric:: Textual History & Projective Axiomatics

1. Euclid's Lost *Porisms*
--------------------------

Of all the lost works of antiquity, Euclid's three books of *Porisms* (*Πορίσματα*) have stimulated the greatest mathematical detective work (from Fermat and Halley to Simson and Chasles). Book VII of the *Synagoge* is our primary surviving source for the nature of a porism, preserving 38 preparatory lemmas.

2. Hilbert's Projective Axiomatics (1899)
-----------------------------------------

In *Grundlagen der Geometrie* (1899), :person:`David Hilbert <hilbert>` showed that Pappus's Theorem (Proposition 139) is equivalent to the **commutativity of multiplication** in the coordinate skew-field of a projective geometry. Desargues's Theorem guarantees that coordinates form a division ring (skew-field), while Pappus's Theorem forces that ring to be a commutative field.

3. The Descartes Connection
---------------------------

In 1637, René Descartes took the multi-line locus problem described by Pappus in Book VII (*Hultsch II, pp. 676–680*) as the explicit test case for his new method of coordinates in *La Géométrie*, thereby inaugurating modern analytic geometry.
