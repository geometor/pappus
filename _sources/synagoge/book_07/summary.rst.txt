1. Analysis vs. Synthesis (*Hultsch II pp. 634–636*)
----------------------------------------------------

* **Analysis** (*ἀνάπαλιν λύσις*): Assumes the sought proposition or construction as already accomplished, tracing implications backward until arriving at an admitted first principle, axiom, or known theorem (*theoretical* vs. *problematic* analysis).
* **Synthesis** (*σύνθεσις*): Reverses the analytical deduction, taking the endpoint of analysis as starting hypothesis and constructing the sought entity forward.

2. The 33-Book Analytical Canon (*Hultsch II p. 636*)
-----------------------------------------------------

Pappus records the complete curriculum of higher Hellenistic analysis across 33 treatises by Euclid (15 books), Apollonius (14 books), Aristaeus (5 books), and Eratosthenes (2 books).

3. Projective Lemmas to Euclid's Porisms
----------------------------------------

* **Cross-Ratio Invariance (Prop. 129)**: $\operatorname{CR}(A, B; C, D) = \operatorname{CR}(A', B'; C', D')$ across central projections from a pencil of four lines.
* **Harmonic Invariance (Prop. 131)**: The diagonals and opposite sides of a complete quadrilateral cut every transversal in harmonic division ($\operatorname{CR} = -1$).
* **Hexagon Collinearity (Props. 138 & 139)**: If the vertices of a hexagon alternate on two lines, the three pairs of opposite sides meet at three collinear points (the Pappus line).
