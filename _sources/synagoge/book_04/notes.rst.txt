Book IV · Philological & Mathematical Notes (*Affine Shear & Inversion*)
========================================================================

.. rubric:: Historical Reception & Transmission

1. The Lost Preface to Book IV
------------------------------

In *Codex Vaticanus Graecus* 218, Book IV begins abruptly with Proposition 1 without an address to Hermodorus or Pandrosion. Philologists (:cite:`Hultsch 1876 <hultsch:1876>`, Vol. I, p. 176) conclude that the introductory folio containing the dedication and table of contents was lost prior to the 10th century.

2. Pappus's Theorem and the Shear Principle
-------------------------------------------

Pappus's proof of Proposition 1 relies entirely on Euclid's shear proposition (*Elements* I.35: parallelograms on the same base and in the same parallels are equal in area). By applying shear twice on each side through the intermediate parallelograms $AB\text{-}HA$ and $AC\text{-}HA$, the areas are transferred without metric computation.

3. The Shoemaker's Knife (*Ἄρβηλος*) in Antiquity
-------------------------------------------------

The term *arbelos* (*ἄρβηλος*, shoemaker's leather-cutting knife) appears first in the :person:`Archimedean <archimedes>` tradition preserved in Arabic by Thābit ibn Qurra (*Liber Assumptorum*, Props. 4–6). Pappus attributes the circle chain proposition to "the ancients" (*οἱ ἀρχαῖοι*), indicating an early Hellenistic origin (likely Archimedes or his immediate circle).
