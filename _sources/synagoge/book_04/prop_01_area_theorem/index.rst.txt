Proposition 1: Generalized Parallelogram Area Theorem
=====================================================

.. rubric:: *Synagoge* Book IV, Ch. 1 / Prop. 1 (:cite:`Hultsch I, pp. 176–178 <hultsch-1876>`) · Original English Translation

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0209
   :page: 176

.. picture:: /_static/constructions/pappus_area_theorem.svg
   :alt: Synagoge Book IV Proposition 1 — Pappus's Parallelogram Area Theorem on a Scalene Triangle

.. enunciation::

   **1.** If :g:`ABC <g1:< A B C >>` be a triangle, and upon its sides :math:`AB`, :math:`AC` there be described any parallelograms whatever, :g:`ABDE <g1:< A B D E >>` and :g:`ACFG <g1:< A C F G >>`,
   and :g:`DE <g1:[ D E ]>` and :g:`FG <g1:[ F G ]>` be produced to meet at :g:`H <g1:H>`, and :g:`HA <g1:[ H A ]>` be joined, the parallelograms :g:`ABDE <g1:< A B D E >>` and :g:`ACFG <g1:< A C F G >>` together become equal to the parallelogram :g:`BCML <g1:< B C M L >>` contained by :math:`BC` and :math:`HA` in an angle equal to both :math:`\angle BAC` and :math:`\angle AHD` together.

.. step::

   For let :g:`HA <g1:[ H A ]>` be produced to :g:`J <g1:J>`, and through :g:`B <g1:B>` and :g:`C <g1:C>` let :g:`BL <g1:[ B L ]>` and :g:`CM <g1:[ C M ]>` be drawn parallel to :math:`HJ`, and let :math:`LM` be joined. Since :math:`BLHA` is a parallelogram, :math:`BL` and :math:`HA` are both equal and parallel.
   Likewise :math:`MC` and :math:`HA` are both equal and parallel, so that :math:`LB` and :math:`MC` are also equal and parallel (:just:`I.30`). Therefore :math:`LM` and :math:`BC` are also equal and parallel (:just:`I.33`); thus :g:`BCML <g1:< B C M L >>` is a parallelogram in the angle :math:`\angle LBC`, that is,

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0211
   :page: 178

.. step::

   equal to both :math:`\angle BAC` and :math:`\angle AHD` together; for :math:`\angle AHD` equals :math:`\angle LBA`.
   And since parallelogram :g:`ABDE <g1:< A B D E >>` equals :math:`LBHA` (for it is on the same base :math:`AB` and in the same parallels :math:`AB` and :math:`DH`, :just:`I.35`), while :math:`LBHA` equals :math:`LBJN` (for it is on the same base :math:`LB` and in the same parallels :math:`LB` and :math:`HJ`), :math:`ABDE` therefore equals :math:`LBJN`.
   For the same reasons :g:`ACFG <g1:< A C F G >>` also equals :math:`NJCM`; therefore the parallelograms :g:`ABDE <g1:< A B D E >>` and :g:`ACFG <g1:< A C F G >>` equal :g:`BCML <g1:< B C M L >>`, that is, the parallelogram contained by :math:`BC` and :math:`HA` in the angle :math:`\angle LBC`, which equals both :math:`\angle BAC` and :math:`\angle AHD` together.
   And this is far more universal than what was proved in the Elements concerning squares on right-angled triangles (:cite:`Euclid's Elements I.47 <euclid-elements>`).
