Proposition 1 · Historical & Modern Affine Commentary
=====================================================

.. rubric:: The Evolution from Pythagorean Metric to Affine Area

1. Generalization Beyond Euclid I.47
------------------------------------

In Euclid's *Elements* I.47, two strict conditions are imposed:

1. The base angle $\angle BAC$ must be a right angle.
2. The figures erected on the sides must be squares.

In *Elements* VI.31, Euclid generalized the figure requirement to arbitrary similar rectilinear figures, but retained the right angle in the triangle. Pappus's Proposition 1 removes **both** restrictions simultaneously: the triangle may be scalene, and the erected figures may be completely dissimilar parallelograms with arbitrary lateral directions.

2. Clairaut's 1741 Rediscovery
------------------------------

In 1741, the French geometer Alexis Clairaut included this theorem in his *Éléments de géométrie*, presenting it as a pedagogical stepping-stone to Pythagoras without noting Pappus's antiquity. Modern historians recognize it as one of the purest theorems of affine geometry in the ancient canon.
