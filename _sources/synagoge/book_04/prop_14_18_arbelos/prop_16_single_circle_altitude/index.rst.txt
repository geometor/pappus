Proposition 16: Altitude Relation for an Inscribed Circle
=========================================================

.. rubric:: *Synagoge* Book IV, Chs. 23–24 / Prop. 16 (:cite:`Hultsch I, pp. 212–218 <hultsch-1876>`) · Original English Translation

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0245
   :page: 212

.. enunciation::

   **23. (16)** Let there be two semicircles :math:`BHG` and :math:`BE\Delta`, and a circle :math:`EZH\Theta` tangent to them, and from its center :math:`A` let the perpendicular :math:`AM` be drawn to the base :math:`B\Gamma` of the semicircles;
   to show that as :math:`MB` is to the radius of circle

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0247
   :page: 214

.. enunciation::

   :math:`EZH\Theta`, so in the first figure the sum of :math:`\Gamma B` and :math:`B\Delta` is to their difference :math:`\Gamma\Delta`, while in the second and third figures the difference of :math:`\Gamma B` and :math:`B\Delta` is to their sum, that is, to :math:`\Gamma\Delta`.

.. step::

   Through :math:`A` let :math:`\Theta Z` be drawn parallel to :math:`B\Gamma`. Since then two circles :math:`BH\Gamma` and :math:`EZH\Theta` touch one another at :math:`H`, and diameters :math:`B\Gamma` and :math:`Z\Theta` in them are parallel, both the line through :math:`H, \Theta, B` and that through :math:`H, Z, \Gamma` will be straight.
   Again, since two circles :math:`BE\Delta` and :math:`EZH\Theta` touch one another at :math:`E`, and parallel diameters :math:`\Theta Z` and :math:`B\Delta` are in them, both the line through :math:`Z, E, B` and that through :math:`\Theta, E, \Delta` will be straight. Let perpendiculars :math:`\Theta K` and :math:`Z\Lambda` also be drawn from points :math:`\Theta` and :math:`Z`.
   Then by the similarity of triangles :math:`BH\Gamma` and :math:`B\Theta K`, as :math:`B\Gamma` is to :math:`BH`, so :math:`B\Theta` is to :math:`BK`, and the rectangle contained by :math:`\Gamma B` and :math:`BK` equals that contained by :math:`HB` and :math:`B\Theta`;
   and by the similarity of triangles :math:`BZ\Lambda` and :math:`BE\Delta`, as :math:`\Delta B` is to :math:`BE`, so :math:`BZ` is to :math:`B\Lambda`, and rectangle :math:`\Delta B\cdot B\Lambda` equals rectangle :math:`ZB\cdot BE`. And rectangle :math:`HB\cdot B\Theta` equals rectangle :math:`ZB\cdot BE`;
   therefore rectangle :math:`\Gamma B\cdot BK` also equals rectangle :math:`\Delta B\cdot B\Lambda`, or if the perpendicular from :math:`Z` falls upon :math:`\Delta`,

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0249
   :page: 216

.. step::

   the square on :math:`B\Delta` (:just:`III.36`). Thus in the first figure as :math:`\Gamma B` is to :math:`B\Delta`, so :math:`\Lambda B` is to :math:`BK`,
   so that the sum of :math:`\Gamma B` and :math:`B\Delta` is to their difference :math:`\Gamma\Delta` as the sum of :math:`\Lambda B` and :math:`BK` is to their difference :math:`K\Lambda`. And :math:`BM` is half of the sum of :math:`\Lambda B` and :math:`BK` (since :math:`KM` equals :math:`M\Lambda`), while :math:`MK` is half of :math:`\Lambda K`;
   therefore as the sum of :math:`\Gamma B` and :math:`B\Delta` is to :math:`\Gamma\Delta`, so :math:`BM` is to :math:`MK`, that is, to the radius of circle :math:`EZH\Theta`. In the second and third figures, since rectangle :math:`\Gamma BK` was shown equal to rectangle :math:`\Delta B\Lambda`, as :math:`\Gamma B` is to :math:`B\Delta`, so :math:`\Lambda B` is to :math:`BK`.
   By composition, as :math:`\Gamma\Delta` is to :math:`\Delta B`, so :math:`K\Lambda` is to :math:`KB`; hence as :math:`\Gamma\Delta` is to the difference of :math:`\Gamma B` and :math:`B\Delta`, so :math:`K\Lambda` is to the difference of :math:`\Lambda B` and :math:`BK`. And half of :math:`K\Lambda` is the radius of circle :math:`EZH\Theta`,
   while :math:`BM` is half of the difference of :math:`\Lambda B` and :math:`BK` because :math:`\Lambda M` equals :math:`MK`; so that as :math:`MB` is to the radius of circle :math:`EZH\Theta`, so in the first figure the sum of :math:`\Gamma B` and :math:`B\Delta` is to their difference :math:`\Gamma\Delta`,
   and in the second and third figures the difference of :math:`\Gamma B` and :math:`B\Delta` is to their sum, that is, to :math:`\Gamma\Delta`.

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0251
   :page: 218

.. step::

   **24.** It is observed at the same time that the rectangle contained by :math:`BK` and :math:`\Lambda\Gamma` equals the square on :math:`AM`.
   For by the similarity of triangles :math:`B\Theta K` and :math:`Z\Lambda\Gamma`, as :math:`BK` is to :math:`K\Theta`, so :math:`Z\Lambda` is to :math:`\Lambda\Gamma`, and rectangle :math:`BK\cdot\Lambda\Gamma` equals rectangle :math:`\Theta K\cdot Z\Lambda`, that is, the square on :math:`AM`. And because as :math:`B\Gamma` is to :math:`\Gamma\Delta`, so :math:`B\Lambda` is to :math:`K\Lambda`,
   the rectangle contained by :math:`B\Gamma` and :math:`K\Lambda`, that is, the diameter of the circle, equals rectangle :math:`B\Lambda\cdot\Delta\Gamma`; and because as :math:`B\Delta` is to :math:`\Gamma\Delta`, so :math:`BK` is to :math:`K\Lambda`, the rectangle contained by :math:`B\Delta` and :math:`K\Lambda`, that is, the diameter of the circle, equals rectangle :math:`BK\cdot\Delta\Gamma`.
