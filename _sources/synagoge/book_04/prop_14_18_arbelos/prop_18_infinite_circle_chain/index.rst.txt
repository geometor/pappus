Proposition 18: Induction for the Infinite Circle Chain
=======================================================

.. rubric:: *Synagoge* Book IV, Chs. 26–27 / Prop. 18 (:cite:`Hultsch I, pp. 224–230 <hultsch-1876>`) · Original English Translation

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0257
   :page: 224

.. picture:: /_static/constructions/pappus_arbelos_chain.svg
   :alt: Synagoge Book IV Proposition 18 — Pappus's Arbelos Tangent Circle Chain (y_n = n d_n)

.. enunciation::

   **26. (18)** These things having been proved beforehand, let semicircle :g:`BHΓ <g3:( O0 A )>` be supposed, and upon its base let any point :g:`Δ <g3:D>` be taken, and upon :math:`B\Delta` and :math:`\Delta\Gamma` let semicircles :g:`BEΔ <g3:( O1 A )>` and :g:`ΔYΓ <g3:( O2 D )>` be described,
   and into the space between the three circumferences, called the :term:`arbelos <arbelos>`, let circles be inscribed tangent to the semicircles and to one another, as many as one may please, such as those about centers :g:`A <g3:K1>`, :g:`Π <g3:K2>`, :g:`O <g3:K3>`, and from their centers let perpendiculars :g:`AM <g3:[ P1 K1 ]>`, :g:`ΠN <g3:[ P2 K2 ]>`, :g:`OΣ <g3:[ P3 K3 ]>` be drawn upon :math:`B\Gamma`;
   I say that :g:`AM <g3:[ P1 K1 ]>` equals the diameter of the circle about :math:`A`, :g:`ΠN <g3:[ P2 K2 ]>` is double the diameter of the circle about :math:`\Pi`, :g:`OΣ <g3:[ P3 K3 ]>` is triple the diameter of the circle about :math:`O`, and the succeeding perpendiculars are multiples of their own diameters according to the successive numbers exceeding one another by unity.

.. step::

   Let diameter :math:`\Theta Z` be drawn parallel to :math:`B\Gamma`, and perpendiculars

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0259
   :page: 226

.. step::

   :math:`\Theta K` and :math:`Z\Lambda`; then according to what was written before, rectangle :math:`\Gamma B\cdot BK` equals rectangle :math:`\Lambda B\cdot B\Delta`, and rectangle :math:`B\Gamma\cdot \Gamma\Lambda` equals rectangle :math:`K\Gamma\Delta`. And on this account as :math:`BK` is to :math:`K\Lambda`, so :math:`K\Lambda` is to :math:`\Lambda\Gamma`;
   for each ratio is the same as that of :math:`B\Delta` to :math:`\Delta\Gamma` (for since rectangle :math:`\Gamma B\cdot BK` equals rectangle :math:`\Lambda B\cdot B\Delta`, therefore as :math:`\Gamma B` is to :math:`B\Lambda`, so :math:`\Delta B` is to :math:`BK`; by division as :math:`\Gamma\Delta` is to :math:`\Delta B`, so :math:`\Lambda K` is to :math:`KB`; by inversion as :math:`B\Delta` is to :math:`\Delta\Gamma`, so :math:`BK` is to :math:`K\Lambda`;
   again since rectangle :math:`B\Gamma\cdot \Gamma\Lambda` equals rectangle :math:`K\Gamma\cdot\Gamma\Delta`, therefore as :math:`B\Gamma` is to :math:`\Gamma K`, so :math:`\Gamma\Delta` is to :math:`\Gamma\Lambda`; alternately as :math:`B\Gamma` is to :math:`\Gamma\Delta`, so :math:`K\Gamma` is to :math:`\Gamma\Lambda`; by division therefore as :math:`B\Delta` is to :math:`\Delta\Gamma`, so :math:`K\Lambda` is to :math:`\Lambda\Gamma`;
   and as :math:`B\Delta` is to :math:`\Gamma\Delta`, so also was :math:`BK` to :math:`K\Lambda`; therefore as :math:`BK` is to :math:`K\Lambda`, so :math:`K\Lambda` is to :math:`\Lambda\Gamma`). Therefore rectangle :math:`BK\cdot\Lambda\Gamma` equals the square on :math:`K\Lambda`. But rectangle :math:`BK\cdot\Lambda\Gamma` was also shown equal to the square on :math:`AM`; therefore :math:`AM` equals :math:`K\Lambda`, that is, diameter :math:`Z\Theta` of the circle about :math:`A`.
   And since this too

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0261
   :page: 228

.. step::

   was proved before, that as :math:`AM` together with :math:`Z\Theta` is to :math:`Z\Theta`, so :math:`\Pi N` is to the diameter of the circle about :math:`\Pi`, and :math:`AM` together with :math:`Z\Theta` is double :math:`Z\Theta`, :math:`\Pi N` will also be double the diameter of the circle about :math:`\Pi`.
   Therefore :math:`\Pi N` together with the diameter of the circle about :math:`\Pi` is triple the diameter, and :math:`O\Sigma` is in the same ratio to the diameter of the circle about :math:`O`; therefore :math:`O\Sigma` is triple the diameter of the circle about :math:`O`.
   And likewise the perpendicular of the next circle is quadruple its diameter, and the succeeding perpendiculars will be found to be multiples of their respective diameters according to the successive numbers exceeding one another by unity, and this will be demonstrated as occurring without end.

.. step::

   **27.** And if instead of the circumferences :math:`BH\Gamma` and :math:`\Delta Y\Gamma` there be straight lines perpendicular to :math:`B\Delta`, as holds in the third figure, the same things will occur for the inscribed circles; for directly the perpendicular from center :math:`A` upon :math:`B\Delta` becomes equal to the diameter of the circle about :math:`A`.
   And if circumferences :math:`BH\Gamma` and :math:`BE\Delta` remain, while instead of circumference :math:`\Delta Y\Gamma` there be supposed a straight line :math:`\Delta Z` perpendicular to :math:`B\Gamma` (as holds in the fourth figure), then when :math:`B\Gamma` has to :math:`\Gamma\Delta` a square ratio in numbers, the perpendicular from :math:`A` will be commensurable with

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0263
   :page: 230

.. step::

   the diameter
   of the circle about :math:`A`, and if not, incommensurable. For in general whatever ratio :math:`B\Gamma` has to :math:`\Gamma\Delta`, that ratio in power does :math:`\Delta Z` have to the diameter of the circle about :math:`A`, as is shown next.
   For example, if :math:`B\Gamma` be quadruple :math:`\Gamma\Delta` in length, :math:`\Delta Z`, that is, the perpendicular from :math:`A`, becomes double in length the diameter of the circle about :math:`A`, and that from :math:`\Pi` triple, and that from :math:`O` quadruple, and so on according to the successive numbers.
