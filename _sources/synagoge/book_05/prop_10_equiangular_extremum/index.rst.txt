Propositions 8–10: Equilateral & Equiangular Polygon Extremum
=============================================================

.. rubric:: *Synagoge* Book V, Chs. 15–19 (:cite:`Hultsch I, pp. 328–334 <hultsch-1876>`) · Original English Translation

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0361
   :page: 328

.. enunciation::

   **15. 8.** Let :math:`AEB` and :math:`\Gamma\Delta Z` be isosceles triangles upon unequal bases :math:`AB` and :math:`\Gamma\Delta`, let :math:`AE` equal :math:`\Gamma Z`, and let :math:`AB` be greater than :math:`\Delta\Gamma` (so the triangles are dissimilar); it is required to construct upon :math:`AB` and :math:`\Gamma\Delta` similar isosceles triangles such that their four sides together equal :math:`AEB` and :math:`\Gamma Z\Delta` together.

.. step::

   For let straight line :math:`H\Theta` be set out equal to :math:`AEB` and :math:`\Gamma Z\Delta`, let it be cut at :math:`K` so that as :math:`HK` is to :math:`K\Theta`, so is base :math:`AB` to :math:`\Gamma\Delta`, and let each of :math:`HK` and :math:`K\Theta` be bisected at points :math:`\Lambda` and :math:`M`.
   Since, then, :math:`H\Theta` is greater than :math:`AB` and :math:`\Gamma\Delta` together (because :math:`AEB` and :math:`\Gamma Z\Delta` are also), and as :math:`AB` is to :math:`\Gamma\Delta`, so is :math:`HK` to :math:`K\Theta`, :math:`HK` is also greater than :math:`AB`, and :math:`K\Theta` than :math:`\Gamma\Delta`.
   And each is bisected; therefore of :math:`AB`, :math:`H\Lambda`, and :math:`\Lambda K`, any two taken together are greater than the remaining one. Likewise also for :math:`\Gamma\Delta`, :math:`KM`, and :math:`M\Theta`.
   Let triangle :math:`A\Xi B` be constructed out of :math:`AB`, :math:`H\Lambda`, and :math:`\Lambda K` (for it is manifest that its sides fall outside :math:`AEB` because :math:`H\Lambda K` are greater than :math:`AEB`; for :math:`AEB` is half of :math:`H\Theta`, since :math:`AEB` equals :math:`\Gamma Z\Delta`,
   and the four straight lines together equal :math:`H\Theta`, while :math:`HK` is greater than half of :math:`H\Theta`), and out of :math:`\Gamma\Delta` and :math:`KM\Theta` let :math:`\Gamma N\Delta` be constructed (for likewise

   they fall inside).
   And it is manifest that the triangles will be similar, since as :math:`AB` is to :math:`\Gamma\Delta`, so is :math:`HK` to :math:`K\Theta`, and their halves, both :math:`H\Lambda` to :math:`KM` and :math:`\Lambda K` to :math:`M\Theta`, and the equal lines constructed from them, :math:`A\Xi` to :math:`\Gamma N` and :math:`B\Xi` to :math:`\Delta N`.

.. step::

   **16.** [Now triangle :math:`AEB` becomes sometimes greater than triangle :math:`\Gamma Z\Delta`, sometimes less, and sometimes equal to it. For let :math:`\Pi P\Sigma` be a triangle having :math:`\Pi P` equal to :math:`Z\Gamma`, :math:`P\Sigma` to :math:`\Delta Z`, and :math:`\Pi\Sigma` to :math:`\Gamma\Delta`;
   therefore triangles :math:`\Gamma Z\Delta` and :math:`\Pi P\Sigma` are equal and similar to one another. Since, then, :math:`AB` is greater than :math:`\Gamma\Delta`, that is, than :math:`\Pi\Sigma`, and :math:`AE` and :math:`EB` equal :math:`\Pi P` and :math:`P\Sigma` (since they equal :math:`\Gamma Z` and :math:`Z\Delta` each to each), :math:`\angle AEB` is greater than :math:`\angle \Pi P\Sigma`
   (since it is also greater than :math:`\angle \Gamma Z\Delta`). Let :math:`\angle \Pi PT` be placed equal to :math:`E`, and :math:`PT` to :math:`P\Sigma`, and let :math:`\Pi T` be joined; therefore triangle :math:`\Pi PT` is equal and similar to triangle :math:`AEB`. Let :math:`\Pi\Sigma\Upsilon` be produced, let :math:`\Pi\Upsilon` equal :math:`AB`, and let :math:`P\Upsilon` be joined;
   therefore :math:`P\Upsilon` is greater than :math:`P\Sigma` and :math:`PT` (for each of :math:`\Pi P` and :math:`PT` equals :math:`P\Sigma`). Triangle :math:`\Pi PT`, then, is equal and similar to :math:`AEB`, and to :math:`\Pi P\Sigma` triangle :math:`\Pi PT` is either equal, if :math:`T\Sigma` be parallel to :math:`P\Pi` and alternate angles :math:`\angle P\Pi T` and :math:`\angle \Pi T\Sigma` be equal
   (for the triangles are upon the same base

   :math:`P\Pi` and in the same parallels :math:`P\Pi` and :math:`T\Sigma`), or greater than it, if :math:`T\Upsilon` be parallel to :math:`P\Pi` and :math:`\angle \Pi T\Upsilon` equal alternate angle :math:`\angle P\Pi T`
   (for again :math:`\Pi PT` becomes equal to :math:`P\Upsilon\Pi`, and :math:`\Pi P\Upsilon` is greater than :math:`\Pi P\Sigma`, so :math:`\Pi PT` is also greater than :math:`\Pi P\Sigma`), or if :math:`T\Phi` be parallel to :math:`P\Pi`, through equal alternate angles :math:`\angle P\Pi T` and :math:`\angle \Pi T\Phi`, :math:`\Pi PT` equals :math:`P\Pi\Phi`,

.. step::

   **17.** The remainder of the postponement [lacuna]

.. enunciation::

   **18. 9.** With these theorems prefixed, we shall prove the proposition before us, namely that among :term:`isoperimetric <isoperimetros>` rectilinear figures having equal numbers of sides, the greatest is both equilateral and equiangular.

.. step::

   For let polygon :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta E` be the greatest of those :term:`isoperimetric <isoperimetros>` with it and having the same number of sides; I say that it is equilateral. If not, let :math:`AB` and :math:`B\Gamma` be unequal if possible, let :math:`A\Gamma` be joined, and upon it let isosceles triangle :math:`AZ\Gamma` be constructed
   so that :math:`AZ` and :math:`Z\Gamma` together equal :math:`AB` and :math:`B\Gamma` together by the fourth theorem. Since it was shown three propositions back that among isoperimetric triangles upon the same base the isosceles is greatest, triangle :math:`AZ\Gamma` is greater than :math:`AB\Gamma`. Adding quadrilateral :math:`A\Gamma\Delta E` in common,
   there will be a region :math:`Z\Gamma\Delta EA`, isoperimetric with the greatest :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta E` and having the same number of sides, yet greater than it, which is impossible; therefore :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta E` is equilateral. And it is manifest that the more equilateral is always

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0367
   :page: 334

.. step::

   greater; for the more isosceles is also always greater, as was proved three propositions back.

.. enunciation::

   **19. 10.** I say further that polygon :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta E` is also equiangular.

.. step::

   If not, let :math:`\angle B` be greater than :math:`\angle \Delta` if possible. Then straight line :math:`A\Gamma` is also greater than :math:`\Gamma E` (for :math:`AB`, :math:`B\Gamma`, :math:`\Gamma\Delta`, and :math:`\Delta E` are equal).
   Upon the unequal bases :math:`A\Gamma` and :math:`\Gamma E` let similar isosceles triangles :math:`AZ\Gamma` and :math:`\Gamma HE` be constructed, as was shown one proposition back, having their sides :math:`AZ\Gamma` and :math:`\Gamma HE` together equal to :math:`AB\Gamma` and :math:`\Gamma\Delta E` together. Then the constructed triangles :math:`AZ\Gamma` and :math:`\Gamma HE` together will be greater than the original :math:`AB\Gamma` and :math:`\Gamma\Delta E`;
   for this also was proved two propositions back. Adding triangle :math:`A\Gamma E` in common, the same absurdity will follow; for :math:`AZ\Gamma HE` will be greater than :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta E`, which is greatest and isoperimetric with it. Therefore polygon :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta E` is also equiangular;
   therefore among :term:`isoperimetric <isoperimetros>` rectilinear figures having equal numbers of sides, the greatest is both equilateral and equiangular.

   And it is clear that greatest of all isoperimetric figures is the circle, since it was shown greater than the regular isoperimetric figure, which is both equilateral and equiangular.
