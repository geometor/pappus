Propositions 3–4: Area of the Circle & Triangle Construction Lemma
==================================================================

.. rubric:: *Synagoge* Book V, Chs. 6–10 (:cite:`Hultsch I, pp. 312–318 <hultsch-1876>`) · Original English Translation

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0345
   :page: 312

.. enunciation::

   **6. 3.** Now that the rectangle contained by the circumference of the circle and its radius is double the circle

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0347
   :page: 314

.. step::

   :person:`Archimedes <archimedes>` has demonstrated; nevertheless this shall also be proved next, so that we may not stand in need of the Archimedean treatise for the sake of this single theorem alone.
   For let :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta` be a circle, and let region :math:`Z` be half of the rectangle contained by its circumference and its radius; I say that region :math:`Z` equals circle :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta`.

.. step::

   **7.** For first, if possible, let it be less.
   Then it is possible, following the method in the twelfth book of the *Elements* (:just:`XII.2`), to inscribe in circle :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta` a polygon such that the inscribed polygon is greater than region :math:`Z`, if a square be first inscribed in the circle and the arcs of the remaining segments be continually bisected until segments remain less than the excess by which circle :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta` exceeds region :math:`Z`.
   Let it be inscribed, and let it be :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta E`, and from center :math:`H` let perpendicular :math:`H\Theta` be drawn to one side of polygon :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta E`, namely :math:`\Gamma\Delta`. Now since the circumference of circle :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta` is greater than the perimeter of the polygon :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta E` of whatever number of sides,
   and the radius is greater than :math:`H\Theta`, the rectangle contained by the circumference of circle :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta` and the radius is greater than the rectangle contained by the perimeter of polygon :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta E` and :math:`H\Theta`. Taking halves, region :math:`Z` is greater than polygon :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta E`, which is impossible;
   for it was supposed less; therefore circle :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta` is not greater than region :math:`Z`.

.. step::

   **8.** I say further that neither is it less. For if possible, let it be less; then it is possible to circumscribe a polygon about circle :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta` such that region :math:`Z` is greater than the circumscribed polygon,
   if a square be first circumscribed about the circle and, the remaining arcs being continually bisected, tangents be drawn

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0349
   :page: 316

.. step::

   until segments of the outer figures be left over that are less than the excess by which region :math:`Z` exceeds circle :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta`; for that this is possible has been proved.
   Let the polygon therefore be circumscribed as stated, and let it be :math:`K\Lambda MN\Xi`, and from center :math:`H` let :math:`HO` be joined to one of the points of contact, namely :math:`O`. Since the perimeter of polygon :math:`K\Lambda MN\Xi` is greater than the circumference of circle :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta`,
   the rectangle contained by the perimeter of polygon :math:`K\Lambda MN\Xi` and :math:`HO` is greater than the rectangle contained by the circumference of circle :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta` and :math:`HO`. Taking halves, polygon :math:`K\Lambda MN\Xi` is greater than region :math:`Z`, which is impossible; for it was supposed less. Therefore region :math:`Z` is not greater than circle :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta`.
   And it was shown that neither is it less; therefore it is equal. And the rectangle contained by the circumference of circle :math:`AB\Gamma\Delta` and the radius of the circle is double region :math:`Z`.

.. step::

   **9. 4.** Now the circle becomes greater not only than regular plane figures, which are both equilateral and equiangular,
   but also than unequilateral and non-equiangular figures, whenever it has the same perimeter as they. For it will be proved that among :term:`isoperimetric <isoperimetros>` polygonal figures having the same number of sides, the greatest is both equilateral and equiangular. First, then, we shall set down beforehand the theorems assumed for its demonstration.

.. step::

   **10.** Let :math:`AB\Gamma` be a triangle having :math:`AB` greater than :math:`B\Gamma`, and let :math:`E` be a straight line which is less than :math:`AB` and greater than :math:`B\Gamma`: it is possible to construct upon :math:`A\Gamma` two straight lines such that both together equal :math:`AB` and :math:`B\Gamma`, and one of them equals :math:`E`.

.. folio:: hultsch_1876_vol1:0351
   :page: 318

.. step::

   For let :math:`Z` be the amount by which :math:`AB` and :math:`B\Gamma` exceed :math:`E`; then :math:`Z` is less than :math:`AB` (because :math:`AB` and :math:`B\Gamma` together equal :math:`E` and :math:`Z`, of which :math:`E` is greater than :math:`\Gamma B`),
   and greater than :math:`\Gamma B` (since again :math:`AB` and :math:`B\Gamma` together equal :math:`E` and :math:`Z`, of which :math:`E` is less than :math:`AB`). Since, then, :math:`AB` and :math:`B\Gamma` are greater than :math:`A\Gamma`, :math:`E` and :math:`Z` are therefore also greater than :math:`A\Gamma`.
   And since :math:`A\Gamma` and :math:`\Gamma B` are greater than :math:`AB`, and :math:`E` is greater than :math:`\Gamma B` while :math:`Z` is less than :math:`AB`, far more are :math:`A\Gamma` and :math:`E` greater than :math:`Z`. Likewise, since :math:`A\Gamma` and :math:`\Gamma B` are greater than :math:`AB`,
   while :math:`Z` is greater than :math:`\Gamma B` and :math:`E` is less than :math:`AB`, far more are :math:`A\Gamma` and :math:`Z` greater than :math:`E`. Therefore it is possible to construct a triangle from :math:`A\Gamma`, :math:`E`, and :math:`Z`. Let :math:`A\Gamma\Delta` be constructed [lacuna] [and it is manifest that if :math:`E` and :math:`Z` are equal, triangle :math:`A\Gamma\Delta` will be isosceles, while if unequal, the greater of them will equal :math:`\Gamma\Delta`].
