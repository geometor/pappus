Center of Gravity and Proposition 1: Triangle Medians
=====================================================

.. rubric:: *Synagoge* Book VIII, Chapter 5 & Proposition 1 (:cite:`Hultsch III, pp. 1030–1038 <hultsch:1878>`) · Critical English Edition

.. folio:: 0039
   :page: 1030

At the foundation of all rational mechanics stands Pappus's purely geometric definition (*Ch. 5, Hultsch III p. 1030*):

    *"We need not examine at present what 'the heavy' and 'the light' are in their ultimate essence, nor why bodies move upward or downward; rather, let us define the **center of gravity** (:term:`κέντρον βάρους <kentron-barous>`) of each body to be **a certain point situated within it, from which, if the weight be conceived as suspended, it remains at rest while borne and preserves its original orientation**, neither tilting nor turning in any revolution."*

From this intrinsic definition—without invoking physical conjecture—Pappus deduces geometrically in **Proposition 1** (*Hultsch III, pp. 1032–1038*) the center of gravity of rods, parallelograms, and triangles via median concurrency and proportional subdivision.
