geometor.pappus.pascal
======================

.. py:module:: geometor.pappus.pascal

.. autoapi-nested-parse::

   Symbolic DAG constructions and combinatorics for Blaise Pascal's *Hexagrammum Mysticum* (1640).

   Generalizes Pappus's Hexagon Theorem from a degenerate line-pair $a \cup b$ to six
   points inscribed on a smooth conic $\mathcal{C}$ (circle or ellipse):

   - **Pascal's Mystic Hexagram** (*Essai pour les coniques*, Feb. 1640): for any cyclic
     6-tuple $\langle P_{c_0}, P_{c_1}, P_{c_2}, P_{c_3}, P_{c_4}, P_{c_5} \rangle$ on a conic,
     opposite side intersections $M_{03}, M_{14}, M_{25}$ lie on a single **Pascal line**.
   - **Bi-Triangle** :math:`S_3` **Steiner Family**: partitioning the 6 conic vertices into two
     complementary triangles $\triangle_A$ and $\triangle_B$ generates 6 alternating hexagons
     whose 3 even (:math:`A_3`) Pascal lines concur at :math:`G_+` and whose 3 odd
     (:math:`S_3 \setminus A_3`) Pascal lines concur at :math:`G_-`—directly generalizing
     Pappus's two Star points :math:`(S_+, S_-)`!
   - **Complete 60-Line *Hexagrammum Mysticum* Catalog**: exact projective enumeration of all
     15 chords, 45 diagonal Pascal points, 60 Pascal lines, 20 Jakob Steiner points,
     60 Thomas Kirkman points, and 15 Julius Plücker lines.
   - **Universal Symbolic Proof & Golden Conic**: exact 6-parameter polynomial proof over
     $\mathbb{Z}[t_0, t_1, t_2, t_3, t_4, t_5]$ and Golden Ratio conic perspectivities over
     $\mathbb{Q}(\phi)$.

.. py:function:: canonical_hexagon_cycle(cycle: Sequence[int]) -> Cycle6

   Normalize a 6-vertex permutation up to dihedral rotation and reversal ($D_6$).

   Rotates ``cycle`` so ``0`` appears first and picks the lexicographically smaller
   orientation between ``(0, c1, c2, c3, c4, c5)`` and ``(0, c5, c4, c3, c2, c1)``.

   :param cycle: Permutation of ``(0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5)``.

   :returns: Canonical 6-tuple representative among the $6! / 12 = 60$ distinct hexagons.

   :raises ValueError: If ``cycle`` is not a permutation of ``0..5``.

   Construct Blaise Pascal's *Hexagrammum Mysticum* for six points on a conic.

   :param model: Optional existing :class:`~geometor.model.Model`; created if ``None``.
   :param points: Optional sequence of 6 coconic points; defaults to ``preset``.
   :param cycle: Optional cyclic ordering of ``0..5``; defaults to the preset's ``default_cycle``.
   :param preset: Key in :data:`PASCAL_PRESETS` used when ``points`` is ``None``.
   :param include_conic: If True and carrier circle/ellipse parameters are known, register the conic.
   :param include_hexagon: If True, register the 6-gon polygon ``< P_c0 ... P_c5 >``.
   :param enable_divine: If True, attach :mod:`geometor.divine` golden section hook.

   :returns: Populated :class:`PascalHexagonResult`.

   :raises ValueError: If fewer/more than 6 distinct coconic points are provided or if

.. py:function:: construct_pascal_steiner_family(model: geometor.model.Model | None = None, points: Sequence[PointInput] | None = None, *, preset: str = 'hexagrammum', triangle_a: tuple[int, int, int] = (0, 2, 4), triangle_b: tuple[int, int, int] = (1, 3, 5), include_conic: bool = True, scan_harmonics: bool = True, enable_divine: bool = False) -> PascalSteinerFamilyResult

   Construct the 6-hexagon :math:`S_3` bi-triangle Pascal family and its two Steiner points.

   Partitions the 6 conic vertices into $\triangle_A = (P_{a_0}, P_{a_1}, P_{a_2})$ and
   $\triangle_B = (P_{b_0}, P_{b_1}, P_{b_2})$. Permuting $\triangle_B$ by $\sigma \in S_3$
   forms 6 alternating hexagons $\langle A_0, B_{\sigma_0}, A_1, B_{\sigma_1}, A_2, B_{\sigma_2} \rangle$
   whose 3 even (:math:`A_3`) Pascal lines concur at :math:`G_+` (ID ``"S1"``) and whose
   3 odd (:math:`S_3 \setminus A_3`) Pascal lines concur at :math:`G_-` (ID ``"S2"``).

   :param model: Optional existing :class:`~geometor.model.Model`.
   :param points: Optional sequence of 6 coconic points; defaults to ``preset``.
   :param preset: Key in :data:`PASCAL_PRESETS` used when ``points`` is ``None``.
   :param triangle_a: Indices $(a_0, a_1, a_2)$ of the first inscribed triangle.
   :param triangle_b: Complementary indices $(b_0, b_1, b_2)$ of the second triangle.
   :param include_conic: If True, register the carrier circle or ellipse.
   :param scan_harmonics: If True, scan the configuration for collinear harmonic tetrads.
   :param enable_divine: If True, attach :mod:`geometor.divine` and scan golden sections.

   :returns: Populated :class:`PascalSteinerFamilyResult`.

   :raises ValueError: If ``triangle_a`` and ``triangle_b`` do not partition ``0..5`` or
       points are not coconic.


.. py:function:: enumerate_hexagrammum_mysticum(points: Sequence[PointInput] | None = None, *, preset: str = 'hexagrammum') -> HexagrammumMysticumCatalog

   Enumerate and verify the complete 60-line *Hexagrammum Mysticum* web in $\mathbb{P}^2$.

   Computes in exact homogeneous coordinates:

   1. **15 Chords** $[P_i P_j]$ ($0 \le i < j \le 5$).
   2. **45 Diagonal Pascal Points** $[P_i P_j] \cap [P_k P_l]$ on disjoint vertex pairs.
   3. **60 Pascal Lines** across all $6! / 12 = 60$ cyclic hexagons, verifying 3-point collinearity.
   4. **20 Jakob Steiner Points** ($10$ triangle partitions $\times 2$ :math:`S_3` parities),
      verifying 3-line concurrency at each point.
   5. **60 Thomas Kirkman Points**, verifying 3-line concurrency on each syntheme Kirkman triple.
   6. **15 Julius Plücker Lines**, each carrying 4 of the 20 Steiner points.

   :param points: Optional sequence of 6 coconic points; defaults to ``preset``.
   :param preset: Key in :data:`PASCAL_PRESETS` used when ``points`` is ``None``.

   :returns: Populated :class:`HexagrammumMysticumCatalog`.

   :raises ValueError: If fewer/more than 6 distinct coconic points are given.


.. py:function:: verify_pascal_symbolic() -> sympy.Expr

   Prove Pascal's *Hexagrammum Mysticum* universally over $\mathbb{Z}[t_0, t_1, t_2, t_3, t_4, t_5]$.

   Parameterizes six generic points on the projective conic $X^2 + Y^2 = Z^2$ via
   $P_k = [1 - t_k^2 : 2 t_k : 1 + t_k^2]$, computes the three opposite-side meets
   $M_{03}, M_{14}, M_{25}$ via 3D vector cross products, and expands the $3 \times 3$
   collinearity determinant $\det[M_{03}, M_{14}, M_{25}]$, returning ``0`` identically.

   :returns: Simplified SymPy polynomial expression (identically ``sp.Integer(0)``).
